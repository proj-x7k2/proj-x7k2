# Evaluation of the mitral-aortic velocity time integral ratio for severity assessment in dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease.

**Journal of veterinary cardiology : the official journal of the European Society of Veterinary Cardiology (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=188 (MMVD 167 + 대조군 21) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42722611/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.jvc.2026.08.007
관련 주제: [[topics/cardiac-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
개 myxomatous mitral valve disease(MMVD)에서 mitral regurgitation(MR) 중증도를 체중 대비 regurgitant volume(RVol/kg)으로 정량화하기는 어렵다. 사람 심장학에서 제안된 Doppler 기반 mitral-to-aortic velocity time integral(VTI) ratio(mitral inflow VTI ÷ left ventricular outflow tract VTI)를 MMVD 개 167마리와 대조군 21마리에 적용한 결과, 이 비율은 ACVIM stage가 진행할수록 증가했고(P<0.001) RVol/kg·regurgitant fraction(RF)과 강하게 상관관계를 보였으며(r=0.75 각각, P<0.001) 재현성도 높았다(intraclass correlation coefficient >0.90). 다변량 회귀에서 early diastolic transmitral velocity(E), left atrium-to-aorta ratio, RVol/kg이 이 비율의 독립적 결정 인자로 확인되었다. RF≥50%(중증 MR)를 식별하는 데 있어 이 비율은 ROC 분석상 AUC 0.89로 가장 높은 정확도를 보였다.

## English summary
Quantitative assessment of mitral regurgitation (MR) severity in canine myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD), particularly regurgitant volume normalized to body weight (RVol/kg), remains challenging. This study applied a Doppler-derived mitral-to-aortic velocity time integral (VTI) ratio (mitral inflow VTI divided by left ventricular outflow tract VTI), previously proposed in human cardiology, to 167 dogs with MMVD and 21 controls. The ratio increased with ACVIM stage (P<0.001) and correlated strongly with RVol/kg and regurgitant fraction (RF) (r=0.75 each; P<0.001), with high repeatability (intraclass correlation coefficient >0.90). Multivariable regression identified early diastolic transmitral velocity (E), left atrium-to-aorta ratio, and RVol/kg as independent determinants. The ratio showed the highest accuracy for identifying severe MR (RF≥50%) on ROC analysis (AUC=0.89).

## 임상 시사점
심초음파에서 mitral inflow VTI와 left ventricular outflow tract VTI만 측정하면 계산 가능한 mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio는 재현성이 높고 RVol/kg·RF와 잘 상관하므로, MMVD 중증도 평가·congestive heart failure 위험도 판단 시 기존 지표에 추가할 수 있는 간단한 Doppler 지표로 고려할 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
MMVD 개 167마리와 대조군 21마리를 대상으로 한 연구로, ROC 분석 등 정량적 검증을 거쳤으나 전향적·후향적 설계 여부가 초록에 명확히 기술되지 않았고 저자들도 stage C/D 개체 수가 적다는 한계를 명시했다. 초록에는 구체적 cut-off 값(비율 수치)이 제시되지 않았다.

## 원문 초록
INTRODUCTION/OBJECTIVES: In dogs, myxomatous mitral valve disease is the most commonly acquired cardiac disorder. Quantitative assessment of mitral regurgitation (MR), particularly regurgitant volume normalized to body weight (RVol/kg), remains challenging. A Doppler-derived mitral-to-aortic velocity time integral (VTI) ratio (calculated as mitral inflow VTI) divided by left ventricular outflow tract VTI]) has been proposed in humans to stratify MR severity. This study evaluated its association with MR severity, American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine stage, and congestive heart failure (CHF).

ANIMALS, MATERIALS AND METHODS: We evaluated 167 dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease and 21 controls. Echocardiography included mitral inflow VTI, left ventricular outflow tract VTI, and RVol/kg. Repeatability was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients. Receiver operating characteristic analysis was used to evaluate performance for detecting CHF and severe MR defined by a regurgitant fraction (RF) ≥50%.

RESULTS: The mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio increased with stage (P<0.001) and correlated with RVol/kg and RF (r=0.75 each; P<0.001), with high repeatability (intraclass correlation coefficient >0.90). Multivariable regression identified early diastolic transmitral velocity (E), left atrium-to-aorta ratio, and RVol/kg as independent determinants of the mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio. Logistic regression to detect CHF (current and past) showed the association between the mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio and CHF. In the receiver operating characteristic curve analysis, the mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio showed the highest accuracy for identifying an RF ≥ 50% (area under the curve=0.89).

STUDY LIMITATIONS: Few dogs were in stage C/D.

CONCLUSIONS: The mitral-to-aortic VTI ratio is a reproducible Doppler index associated with MR severity and American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine stage and may help indicate clinically relevant RF thresholds.
