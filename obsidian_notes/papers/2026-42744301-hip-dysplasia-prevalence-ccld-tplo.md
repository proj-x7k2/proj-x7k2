# Prevalence of canine hip dysplasia and its association with cranial cruciate ligament deficiency in dogs undergoing tibial plateau levelling osteotomy: a retrospective study.

**New Zealand veterinary journal (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=466 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42744301/) · DOI: 10.1080/00480169.2026.2728624
관련 주제: [[topics/musculoskeletal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
2021년 9월~2022년 12월 단일 3차 진료기관에서 TPLO를 받은 개 466마리의 수술 전 골반 방사선을 후향적으로 검토해 canine hip dysplasia(CHD)의 방사선학적 유병률과 osteoarthritis(OA) 중증도, cranial cruciate ligament deficiency(CCLD)와의 관계를 분석했다. CHD의 방사선학적 유병률은 36.3%(169/466)였고, dysplastic 개의 74.0%에서 방사선학적 OA가 확인되었으며 CHD 중증도가 높을수록 OA 중증도도 강하게 증가했다(OR 22.91/단계). CHD 중증도가 높을수록 가장 심하게 침범된 고관절의 반대쪽 stifle에서 CCLD가 발생할 가능성이 더 높았으나(OR 3.70/단계), CHD 중증도는 반대쪽 CCLD 발생까지 걸리는 시간과는 뚜렷한 연관이 없었다(HR 0.93/단계, p=0.52).

## English summary
This retrospective study reviewed preoperative pelvic radiographs from 466 dogs treated with TPLO for cranial cruciate ligament deficiency (CCLD) between September 2021 and December 2022 to describe the radiographic prevalence and severity of canine hip dysplasia (CHD) and its association with osteoarthritis (OA) and CCLD laterality. Radiographic CHD prevalence was 36.3% (169/466), with 74.0% of dysplastic dogs showing radiographic OA; CHD severity was strongly associated with OA severity (OR 22.91 per category). Increasing CHD severity was associated with higher odds of CCLD occurring contralateral to the most severely dysplastic hip (OR 3.70 per category), but showed little association with time to opposite-side CCLD (HR 0.93, p=0.52).

## 임상 시사점
CCLD로 TPLO를 계획할 때 고관절 방사선을 함께 평가하면, CHD가 심할수록 반대쪽 stifle에서 CCLD가 발생할 위험이 높다는 정보를 보호자 상담과 장기 관리 계획(체중 관리, 조기 개입 논의 등)에 활용할 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 3차 진료기관에서 TPLO를 받은 개 466마리의 의무기록을 후향적으로 검토한 관찰 연구로, 다변량 로지스틱·순서형 모델과 Cox 비례위험모델을 사용했다. 저자들 스스로 "관찰 연구 결과이므로 해석에 주의가 필요하다"고 명시했으며, 이는 TPLO를 받으러 온 환자군(referral population)에 국한된 결과라 일반 개 인구집단의 CHD-CCLD 관계로 확대 해석하기는 어렵다.

## 원문 초록
AIMS: To describe the radiographic prevalence and severity of canine hip dysplasia (CHD) in dogs undergoing tibial plateau levelling osteotomy (TPLO) for cranial cruciate ligament deficiency (CCLD); to evaluate the association between CHD and osteoarthritis (OA) severity; to determine whether the level of CHD or OA in the most severely affected hip was associated with ipsilateral vs contralateral CCLD; and for dogs that had one documented case of CCLD, whether the level of CHD severity in the most severely affected hip was associated with time to opposite-side CCLD.

METHODS: Medical records from 466 client-owned dogs treated with TPLO for CCLD at a single referral centre between September 2021 and December 2022 were reviewed retrospectively. Pre-operative radiographs were assessed for CHD and OA severity. The relationship between the most severely affected hip and operated stifle was recorded as ipsilateral or contralateral. Prevalence estimates, breed-specific distribution analyses, multivariable logistic and ordinal models, and Cox proportional hazards models were used to evaluate associations between CHD severity, OA severity, ipsilateral vs contralateral CCLD, and time to opposite-side CCLD.

RESULTS: The radiographic prevalence of CHD was 169/466 (36.3 (95% CI = 31.9-40.6)%). Among dysplastic dogs, 74.0% showed radiographic evidence of OA. CHD severity was strongly associated with OA severity (OR per one-category increase in CHD severity score 22.91 (95% CI = 14.87-35.32); p < 0.001). Increasing CHD severity was associated with higher odds of CCLD occurring in the stifle contralateral to the most severely affected hip (OR per one-category increase in CHD severity score 3.70 (95% CI = 1.88-7.91); p < 0.001). CHD severity showed little evidence of association with time to opposite-side CCLD (HR per one-category increase in CHD severity score 0.93 (95% CI = 0.73-1.17); p = 0.52).

CONCLUSIONS: CHD was common among dogs undergoing TPLO for CCLD in this referral population. Compared to dogs with mild CHD, dogs with more severe CHD were more likely to develop CCLD in the stifle contralateral to the most severely affected hip. However, in dogs that had been treated for CCLD, CHD severity was not associated with time to any subsequent opposite-side CCLD. These observational findings should be interpreted with caution.

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: Identification of CHD in dogs undergoing TPLO may assist clinical assessment of pelvic limb function and long-term management. Prospective studies are needed to clarify the relationships between CHD and CCLD concerning CCLD rupture relative to the most dysplastic hip.
