# Predominant mitral valve morphology in dogs at different stages of myxomatous mitral valve disease: toward a standardized nomenclature.

**Journal of veterinary cardiology : the official journal of the European Society of Veterinary Cardiology (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=1108 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42760156/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.jvc.2026.08.004
관련 주제: [[topics/cardiac-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
개 myxomatous mitral valve disease(MMVD)는 가장 흔한 심장질환이지만 mitral valve의 형태학적 특징은 중증도 평가에 체계적으로 반영되지 않아 왔다. 이 후향적 연구는 체중 1~15kg, ACVIM stage B1(595)·B2(314)·C/D(199)로 진단된 개 1108마리의 심초음파를 재검토해, right parasternal·left apical view에서 leaflet involvement를 Barlow의 형태학적 기술과 Carpentier 분류의 leaflet motion 원리에 따라 billow, prolapse, isolated flail, prolapse with flail로 분류했다. Anterior leaflet 침범이 모든 stage에서 우세했으나 stage C/D에서는 bileaflet involvement가 증가했다. Billow 형태는 stage가 진행할수록 유의하게 감소했고(B1 79%, B2 17%, C/D 5%, P<0.001), prolapse와 isolated flail은 B2·C/D에서 B1보다 유의하게 흔했으며(P<0.001), prolapse with flail은 B1 5%→B2 25%→C/D 56%로 단계적으로 증가했다(P<0.001).

## English summary
Myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD) is the most common heart disease in dogs, but morphological features of the mitral valve have not been systematically incorporated into severity assessment. This retrospective study reviewed echocardiographic studies from 1108 dogs (1-15 kg) with MMVD (ACVIM stage B1=595, B2=314, C/D=199), classifying leaflet involvement on right parasternal and left apical views as billow, prolapse, isolated flail, or prolapse with flail, based on Barlow's morphologic descriptions and Carpentier classification principles of leaflet motion. Anterior leaflet involvement predominated at all stages, though bileaflet involvement increased in stage C/D. Billow morphology decreased significantly across stages (B1: 79%; B2: 17%; C/D: 5%; P<0.001); prolapse and isolated flail were significantly more common in B2 and C/D than B1 (P<0.001); prolapse with flail increased progressively from B1 (5%) to B2 (25%) to C/D (56%) (P<0.001).

## 임상 시사점
MMVD 심초음파 판독 시 leaflet involvement 패턴(billow → prolapse → isolated flail/prolapse with flail)을 함께 기술하면, 사람 심장학의 표준화된 형태학적 분류와 정렬되는 정보로서 stage 진행·예후 논의에 참고할 수 있는 추가 소견이 된다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일기관 후향적 연구로 종단적 추적 관찰은 이루어지지 않았고, 저자들도 advanced imaging modality(예: 3D 심초음파)는 평가하지 않았다고 명시했다. 형태학적 분류가 임상 결과(생존, CHF 발생)와 직접 연관되는지는 이 연구만으로는 알 수 없다.

## 원문 초록
INTRODUCTION/OBJECTIVES: Myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD) is the most common heart disease in dogs. Echocardiographic markers of cardiac remodeling are used to assess disease severity, but morphological features of the mitral valve are not systematically considered. Conversely, standardized morphological classification schemes are established in human cardiology and are integral to prognosis and therapeutic planning. Our objective was to adapt and apply a mitral valve morphological classification scheme to canine MMVD across various stages.

ANIMALS, MATERIAL AND METHODS: A total of 1108 dogs weighing 1-15 kg with an echocardiographic diagnosis of MMVD (American College of Veterinary Internal Medicine stage B1 = 595; B2 = 314; C/D = 199) were included in the study. Echocardiographic studies were retrospectively reviewed. Leaflet involvement was identified using right parasternal and left apical views and classified as billow, prolapse, isolated flail, or prolapse with flail based on established morphologic descriptions by Barlow and principles of leaflet motion derived from the Carpentier classification.

RESULTS: Anterior leaflet involvement predominated all stages, though bileaflet involvement increased in stage C/D. Billow morphology decreased significantly across stages (B1: 79%; B2: 17%; C/D: 5%; P<0.001). Prolapse and isolated flail were significantly more common in stages B2 and C/D than in stage B1 (P<0.001). Prolapse with flail increased progressively from stage B1 (5%) to B2 (25%) to C/D (56%) (P<0.001).

STUDY LIMITATIONS: This was a retrospective, single-center study with no longitudinal follow-up. Advanced imaging modalities were not evaluated.

CONCLUSIONS: Mitral valve morphology in dogs with MMVD varies systematically with disease stage, and adoption of a standardized morphological classification scheme will align veterinary cardiology with established human frameworks.
