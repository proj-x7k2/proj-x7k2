# Contrast-Enhanced Ultrasonography of Orbital and Intraocular Pathologies in 24 Dogs and 6 Cats.

**Veterinary ophthalmology (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=30 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42897728/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.crad.2017.03.026
관련 주제: [[topics/head-neck-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
B-mode ultrasonography 후 sulfur hexafluoride 조영제로 contrast-enhanced ultrasonography(CEUS)를 시행한 개 24마리·고양이 6마리를 후향적으로 평가했다. 병변은 orbital neoplastic, orbital inflammatory, intraocular neoplastic, intraocular inflammatory, retinal detachment의 5개 군으로 분류했고, contrast intensity·enhancement pattern·perfusion type·wash-in/wash-out 시간을 qualitative하게 평가했다. 모든 retinal detachment 증례에서 persistent vascularization이 관찰되었고, 종양성 병변에서는 CEUS가 lesion localization, 주변 조직과의 관계, perfusion 평가를 향상시켰다. 반면 abscess, myositis, steatitis 같은 염증성 병변에서는 B-mode 대비 추가 진단 가치가 제한적이었다.

## English summary
Thirty animals (24 dogs, 6 cats) with orbital or intraocular disease underwent B-mode ultrasonography followed by sulfur hexafluoride CEUS, and the examinations were evaluated retrospectively. Lesions were grouped as orbital neoplastic, orbital inflammatory, intraocular neoplastic, intraocular inflammatory, or retinal detachment, and qualitative perfusion parameters were assessed. Vascularization was detected in all pathologies with variable enhancement; persistent vascularization was seen in all retinal detachments. CEUS improved localization and perfusion assessment of neoplastic lesions, but added limited value over B-mode for inflammatory lesions. The authors call for prospective studies.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVE: Contrast-enhanced ultrasonography (CEUS) is a noninvasive and relatively safe imaging technique to assess ocular perfusion, showing high sensitivity and specificity for tumor characterization in human oncology, while reports on its application in canine and feline diseased eyes are limited. This study aimed to describe the CEUS features of orbital and intraocular pathologies in dogs and cats in order to evaluate its diagnostic value in detecting and characterizing various lesions.

ANIMAL STUDIED: CEUS examinations of 30 animals (24 dogs, 6 cats) were evaluated retrospectively.

PROCEDURES: Following B-mode ultrasonography, CEUS examinations utilizing sulfur hexafluoride were performed. Pathologies were categorized in one of the following groups: orbital neoplastic lesions, orbital inflammatory lesions, intraocular neoplastic lesions, intraocular inflammatory lesions and retinal detachment. Qualitative perfusion parameters, contrast intensity, enhancement pattern, perfusion type and time to wash-in and wash-out were evaluated.

RESULTS: CEUS detected vascularization in orbital and intraocular pathologies, with variable enhancement characteristics. Persistent vascularization was observed in all cases of retinal detachment. In orbital and intraocular neoplastic lesions, CEUS improved lesion localization, assessment of interactions with surrounding tissues and tissue perfusion. For inflammatory pathologies such as abscesses, myositis, and steatitis, the additional diagnostic value of CEUS compared to B-mode ultrasonography was limited.

CONCLUSION: Findings indicated that CEUS could identify a variety of orbital and intraocular pathologies and provided additional diagnostic information to B-mode ultrasonography, particularly for assessing neoplastic lesions and retinal detachments. CEUS has the potential to enhance ophthalmic ultrasonography in differentiating specific pathologies. Prospective studies are warranted to further evaluate its clinical use.
