# Transthoracic lung ultrasound for perioperative assessment of dogs undergoing nonemergency anesthesia: a prospective observational study.

**Veterinary anaesthesia and analgesia (2026)** · 전향적 연구, n=44 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925847/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.vaa.2026.101374
관련 주제: [[topics/thoracic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
사람에서 atelectasis를 시사하는 lung ultrasound(LUS) 소견이 개의 전신마취 후에도 확인되는지, 그리고 임상 변수와 연관되는지를 알아보기 위해 dorsal recumbency로 비응급 수술을 받은 ASA I-II 개 44마리(자발호흡 27, IPPV 17)를 대상으로 마취 직전·직후에 표준화된 LUS를 시행했다. 8 MHz-5 MHz curvilinear probe로 양측 caudal, perihilar, middle, cranial 8개 부위의 4초 cineloop을 얻었고, B-lines, irregular pleural line, subpleural abnormality를 반영한 자체 개발 점수(최대 32점)로 마스킹된 관찰자가 평가했다. LUS 점수는 마취 전 중앙값 1(0-5)에서 마취 후 7(0-18)로 유의하게 증가했다(Hodges-Lehmann 중앙값 증가 6.5점, 95% CI 5.0-8.5, p<0.001). 연령을 포함한 동물·마취·수술·호흡 변수 중 마취 후 점수와 유의하게 연관된 것은 없었다.

## English summary
This prospective, repeated-measures observational study explored whether lung ultrasound (LUS) findings suggestive of atelectasis in humans can be identified in dogs after general anesthesia, and whether they relate to clinical variables. Forty-four ASA I-II dogs undergoing nonemergency surgery in dorsal recumbency (27 spontaneously breathing, 17 with IPPV) had a standardized eight-site LUS exam immediately before and after anesthesia, scored offline by a masked observer (maximum 32 points). LUS scores rose from a median of 1 (0-5) to 7 (0-18) (Hodges-Lehmann increase 6.5, 95% CI 5.0-8.5, p < 0.001). No animal, anesthetic, surgical, or respiratory variable was associated with post-anesthesia scores after multiple-comparison correction.

## 임상 시사점
마취 후 LUS 점수 상승은 postanesthetic atelectasis를 시사하지만, 이 연구는 CT 등 기준표준이나 임상 결과와 대조하지 않았으므로 LUS 소견을 atelectasis로 확진하는 근거로 쓰기는 이르다. 다만 회복 시 저산소혈증 환자에서 B-lines, irregular pleural line 같은 소견을 POCUS로 빠르게 확인해 보는 접근의 가능성을 보여준다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 관찰자가 시행한 단일 시점 전후 비교 관찰 연구로, 점수체계는 이 연구를 위해 개발된 것이며 검증되지 않았다. 마취 후 검사는 FiO2 100%에서 이루어졌고, 초록상 CT·방사선 등 기준표준과의 비교는 없으며, 변수 연관성 분석은 탐색적이고 표본이 작아 검정력이 제한적이다.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVE: To explore whether lung ultrasound (LUS) findings suggestive of atelectasis in humans could be identified in dogs after anesthesia and to evaluate whether these findings were associated with clinical variables.

STUDY DESIGN: Prospective, repeated-measures observational study with masked offline analysis.

ANIMALS: Forty-four client-owned dogs (American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status classification I-II) undergoing nonemergency surgical procedures in dorsal recumbency; 27 breathed spontaneously throughout anesthesia and 17 had intermittent positive-pressure ventilation.

METHODS: Dogs underwent a standardized LUS examination at two time points: immediately pre- (time point 1) and postanesthesia (time point 2). LUS examinations were performed by a single observer trained in a standardized protocol with dogs standing or in sternal recumbency. A curvilinear 8-5 MHz transducer probe was used to capture 4-second cineloops at eight sites (right and left caudal, perihilar, middle, and cranial). Images were assessed by an observer masked to dog and time point using a standardized scoring system (maximum score of 32) developed for the study that considered B-lines (vertical hyperechoic artefacts), irregular pleural line, and subpleural abnormalities. At time point 2, while dogs were receiving an FiO2 of 100%, arterial partial pressure of oxygen was measured using a point-of-care blood gas analyzer on samples collected from the dorsal pedal artery. LUS scores were compared between time points using the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. Animal, anesthetic, surgical, and respiratory variables were assessed for association with time point 2 LUS scores using univariable linear models adjusted for the time point 1 LUS score.

RESULTS: LUS scores increased significantly (p < 0.001) from time point 1 to 2: median (range) 1 (0-5) versus 7 (0-18); Hodges-Lehmann median increase 6.5 points (95% confidence interval 5.0 to 8.5), p < 0.001. Exploratory analyses did not detect an association between time point 2 LUS scores and any assessed variables after correction for multiple comparisons (age, adjusted p = 0.443).

CONCLUSIONS: LUS scores increased in dogs following general anesthesia.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Further investigation of LUS as an accessible and affordable diagnostic method for detecting atelectasis in the perianesthetic period is warranted.
