# Negative-pressure pulmonary edema due to nasopharyngeal polyps in a cat.

**Veterinary Research Forum (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43005954/)
관련 주제: [[topics/thoracic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
5개월령 수컷 Himalayan 고양이가 labored tachypnea와 상부기도 stertor로 내원했다. 흉부·두부 방사선에서 양측 폐야의 amorphous interstitial·alveolar pattern과 soft palate 위쪽(dorsal) nasopharynx를 채우는 soft tissue mass가 확인되어 nasopharyngeal polyp에 의한 negative-pressure pulmonary edema(NPPE)로 판단했다. 산소 및 intravenous furosemide에 잘 반응했고, 수술 전 NPPE가 1회 재발했으나 polyp 제거 후에는 재발하지 않았다. 저자들은 고양이에서 nasopharyngeal polyp과 NPPE의 연관이 처음 보고된 것이라고 밝힌다.

## English summary
A 5-month-old Himalayan cat with labored tachypnea and stertor had diffuse interstitial-to-alveolar lung patterns and a nasopharyngeal soft tissue mass dorsal to the soft palate on radiographs. Negative-pressure pulmonary edema secondary to a nasopharyngeal polyp was diagnosed; the cat responded to oxygen and furosemide, had one recurrence before polyp removal, and none afterwards. The authors state this association had not been previously documented in cats.

## 임상 시사점
어린 고양이에서 inspiratory effort가 두드러지고 심장 비대 없이 폐부종 pattern이 보이면 NPPE를 감별에 넣고, 흉부 방사선과 함께 두부(nasopharynx) 방사선·구강 내 검사로 nasopharyngeal mass를 확인한다. 심인성 부종으로 오인하여 장기 이뇨제 치료로 끌고 가지 않도록 주의한다.

## 원문 초록
A 5-month-old male Himalayan cat presented with labored tachypnea and abnormal inspiratory sounds (stertor) in the upper airways. Chest and head radiography revealed amorphous interstitial and alveolar patterns in both lung fields and a soft tissue mass occupying the nasopharynx dorsal to the soft palate. The owner reported that the cat had continuously made sustained inspiratory efforts, which had become more severe on the day of visit. Based on these findings, we considered that the clinical signs were due to negative-pressure pulmonary edema (NPPE) caused by a nasopharyngeal polyp. The cat responded well to symptomatic treatments, including oxygen therapy and intravenous furosemide, and was discharged with surgical treatment planned at the secondary hospital. Although the cat had one recurrence of NPPE before the polyp was surgically removed, however, not suffered from NPPE after the polyp disappeared. The NPPE is a rare disease in cats, and its association with nasopharyngeal polyps has not been previously documented. Nasopharyngeal polyps located above the larynx can cause NPPE because mouth breathing is uncommon in cats. Veterinarians should consider NPPE in cats exhibiting inspiratory effort, particularly when chest radiographs show signs of pulmonary edema. Careful and detailed interviews with the owners/caregivers about respiratory patterns and nasopharyngeal examinations are essential.
