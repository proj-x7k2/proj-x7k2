# Age-Related Decrease in Interthalamic Adhesion Thickness in Cats Without Apparent Cognitive Dysfunction.

**Animals (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=112 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939247/)
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
4개월~18세 client-owned 고양이 112마리의 MRI를 후향적으로 검토해, 두 명의 맹검 판독자가 transverse T1W·T2W 영상에서 interthalamic adhesion thickness(IAT)를 측정했다. 연령은 IAT와 음의 연관을 보였고(T1W B=-0.071 mm/year, T2W B=-0.067 mm/year; 모두 p<0.001), 뇌 높이·너비, 성별/중성화, 품종군, 스캐너 보정 후와 kitten 또는 brachycephalic 제외 후에도 유지되었다. Senior(≥11세) 고양이는 다른 모든 연령군보다 IAT가 낮았다. 저자들은 개체 수준의 진단 도구가 아니라 집단 수준의 연관이라고 강조했다.

## English summary
In 112 client-owned cats (4 months-18 years) without intracranial lesions or reported cognitive changes, IAT measured on transverse T1W and T2W MRI declined with age (about -0.07 mm/year on both sequences, p<0.001), robust to multivariable adjustment and to excluding kittens or brachycephalic cats. Senior cats had lower IAT than all other life stages. The authors stress this is a group-level association, and that diagnostic value for feline cognitive dysfunction syndrome needs prospective evaluation.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 노화에 따라 interthalamic adhesion이 얇아지는 것은 정상 노화 범위일 수 있으므로, 고령 고양이 MRI에서 얇은 IAT만으로 cognitive dysfunction을 시사하거나 병적 atrophy로 해석하지 않는 것이 안전하다. 개체 수준 cut-off는 제시되지 않았다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 연구의 후향적 MRI 검토로 스캐너가 여러 대이며(보정함), 인지기능은 보호자 보고로만 평가하고 FCDS 확진 고양이 군이 없어 진단 성능은 알 수 없다. Quadratic 모델의 고령에서의 가파른 감소는 T1W에서만 보인 탐색적 소견이다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않는 값이어서 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
Feline cognitive dysfunction syndrome (FCDS) is an age-associated disorder for which objective imaging markers remain limited. This study evaluated the association between age and interthalamic adhesion thickness (IAT) in cats without apparent cognitive dysfunction and described IAT across feline life stages. MRI examinations from 112 client-owned cats aged 4 months to 18 years were retrospectively reviewed. Cats were classified as kittens (<1 year), young (1 to < 7 years), mature (7 to <11 years) and senior (≥11 years). Cats with intracranial lesions or reported behavioral changes suggestive of cognitive dysfunction were excluded. Two blinded observers measured IAT on transverse T1W and T2W images. Analyses included linear regression adjusted for brain height, brain width, sex/neuter status, breed group, and scanner; exploratory quadratic age modeling; and comparisons among life stages. Interobserver agreement was high. Age was negatively associated with IAT on T1W (B = -0.071 mm/year) and T2W images (B = -0.067 mm/year; both p < 0.001). This association persisted after multivariable adjustment (T1W: B = -0.074 mm/year; T2W: B = -0.067 mm/year; both p < 0.001) and after excluding kittens or brachycephalic cats. Senior cats had lower IAT than all other life stages on both sequences. A quadratic term suggested a steeper decline at older ages on T1W images only; this finding remains exploratory. These findings support a group-level association between age and IAT, rather than individual-level diagnostic use. Its potential diagnostic value for FCDS requires prospective evaluation with standardized behavioral assessment and age-matched controls.
