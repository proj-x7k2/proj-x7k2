# Interictal Single-Voxel Proton Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy of the Temporal Lobe in Cats with Idiopathic Epilepsy: A Prospective Case-Control Study.

**Veterinary sciences (2026)** · 전향적 연구, n=40 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42797884/) · DOI: 10.3390/ani15111504
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
전형적으로 뇌 MRI가 정상 소견을 보이는 idiopathic epilepsy(IE) 고양이 20마리와 건강한 대조군 20마리를 대상으로 양측 temporal lobe(mid-hippocampus·amygdala 중심)의 interictal single-voxel 1H-MRS를 시행해 NAA/tCr, NAA/tCho, tCho/tCr, tCho/NAA 비율을 비교했다. 전체적으로는 두 군 간 유의한 대사물질 비율 차이가 없었지만, 우측 temporal lobe NAA/tCr 비율이 IE군에서 대조군보다 유의하게 낮았다(1.32±0.29 vs 1.50±0.20, p<0.05). 발작 시점도 좌측 반구 스펙트럼에 영향을 미쳐, 마지막 발작 이후 경과 시간이 짧을수록 좌측 tCho/tCr이 낮아졌고(Spearman r=-0.48, p=0.035) 최초-최종 발작 간격이 3일 이내인 개체는 그보다 긴 개체보다 좌측 NAA/tCho가 낮고(p=0.032) tCho/NAA가 높았다(p=0.039). 저자들은 겉보기에 정상인 MRI 뒤에 숨은 temporal lobe 대사 이상을 1H-MRS가 검출할 수 있는 보조 도구가 될 수 있다고 결론지었다.

## English summary
Twenty client-owned cats with idiopathic epilepsy (IE) and 20 healthy controls, all with unremarkable conventional MRI, underwent bilateral interictal single-voxel 1H-MRS centred on the mid-hippocampus and amygdala. Overall, no metabolite ratio differed significantly between groups, but the right temporal lobe NAA/tCr ratio was significantly lower in IE cats (1.32 ± 0.29) than controls (1.50 ± 0.20; p < 0.05). Seizure timing modulated the left-hemisphere spectrum: left tCho/tCr correlated negatively with time since the last seizure (Spearman r = -0.48; p = 0.035), and cats with ≤3 days between first and last seizures had lower left NAA/tCho (p = 0.032) and higher left tCho/NAA (p = 0.039) than cats with a longer interval. The authors propose 1H-MRS as a promising non-invasive adjunct for characterising temporal lobe metabolic disturbances in feline IE despite normal-appearing MRI.

## 임상 시사점
MRI가 정상인 고양이 idiopathic epilepsy 증례에서도 1H-MRS로 우측 temporal lobe NAA/tCr 저하 등 대사 이상을 추가로 확인할 수 있어, 정밀 검사가 가능한 시설이라면 원인불명 발작 환자의 병태생리 평가에 MRS 추가를 고려할 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
전향적 case-control 연구로 IE 20마리·대조군 20마리에서 양측 temporal lobe 1H-MRS를 비교했다. 전체 대사물질 비율 차이는 크지 않고 유의한 소견 대부분이 좌우 비대칭적(우측 NAA/tCr, 좌측 발작 시점 연관 지표)으로 나타나 다중비교에 따른 우연한 발견 가능성을 배제하기 어려우며, 발작의 원인(진정한 idiopathic 여부)에 대한 장기 추적이나 조직학적 확인은 없었다.

## 원문 초록
Background: Idiopathic epilepsy (IE) is among the most frequent chronic neurological disorders of cats, but by definition it is associated with an unremarkable conventional brain MRI, leaving its temporal lobe pathophysiology poorly characterised. Objectives: To quantify interictal temporal lobe neurometabolite ratios in cats with IE using bilateral single-voxel 1H-MRS and to compare them with healthy controls, and to explore relationships with seizure timing and signalment. Methods: In this prospective case-control study, 20 client-owned cats with idiopathic epilepsy and 20 healthy control cats underwent bilateral single-voxel 1H-MRS (PRESS; TE 135 ms; 10 × 10 × 10 mm voxel centred on the mid-hippocampus and amygdala). N-acetylaspartate (NAA), total choline (tCho), and total creatine (tCr) were quantified and the NAA/tCr, NAA/tCho, tCho/tCr, and tCho/NAA ratios were compared (independent-samples t-tests; one-way ANOVA; Spearman correlation; α = 0.05). Results: Overall, no metabolite ratio differed significantly between groups across both hemispheres, and no ratio correlated with age, sex, breed, coat length, or neutering status. However, the right temporal lobe NAA/tCr ratio was significantly lower in epileptic cats (1.32 ± 0.29) than in controls (1.50 ± 0.20; p < 0.05). Seizure timing modulated the left-hemisphere spectrum: left tCho/tCr correlated negatively with the interval since the last seizure (Spearman r = -0.48; p = 0.035), and cats with ≤3 days between first and last seizures showed lower left NAA/tCho (0.89 ± 0.23 vs. 1.14 ± 0.24; p = 0.032) and higher left tCho/NAA (1.21 ± 0.40 vs. 0.91 ± 0.17; p = 0.039) than cats with a longer interval. Conclusions: Interictal 1H-MRS detects temporal lobe metabolic disturbances, including a reduced right temporal lobe NAA/tCr ratio suggesting neuronal or metabolic dysfunction and seizure timing-dependent membrane turnover shifts, in cats with normal-appearing MRI. 1H-MRS is therefore a promising non-invasive adjunct for characterising, monitoring, and potentially assisting in the localisation of metabolic abnormalities in feline IE and warrants validation in larger, longitudinally sampled cohorts.
