# A species comparison of videofloroscopic metrics of swallowing in healthy dogs and cats.

**Journal of veterinary internal medicine (2026)** · 전향적 연구(cross-sectional), n=25 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42955828/)
관련 주제: [[topics/abdominal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
임상 증상이 없는 건강한 성체 고양이 13마리와 개 12마리에서 표준화된 videofluoroscopic swallow study(VFSS)를 시행하고 16개 objective metric을 종간 비교했다. 고양이는 개보다 inter-swallow interval이 길고 pharyngeal transit time이 짧았으며, primary peristalsis를 만들기 위해 필요한 pharyngeal swallow 횟수가 더 많았다(P<.05). 또한 고양이에서 esophageal volume accumulation과 distal esophageal transit time 지연이 더 흔했다(P<.05). Physiologic reflux는 고양이 2마리(15.4%), 개 6마리(50%)에서 확인되었고, 고양이 1마리에서는 호흡기·소화기 증상이 없었음에도 기침 없는 aspiration(P-A score 7)이 관찰되었다.

## English summary
Standardized VFSS was performed in 13 healthy cats and 12 healthy dogs and 16 objective metrics were compared between species. Cats had longer inter-swallow intervals, shorter pharyngeal transit times and needed more pharyngeal swallows to generate primary peristalsis, and were more likely to show esophageal volume accumulation and slowed distal esophageal transit (all P<.05). Physiologic reflux occurred in 15.4% of cats vs 50% of dogs. One asymptomatic cat showed occult tracheal aspiration of contrast (P-A score 7) without cough.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 VFSS를 개 기준값으로 판독하면 pharyngeal transit이 짧은 것이나 distal esophageal 지연·volume accumulation을 이상으로 과대해석할 수 있으므로, 고양이 정상 범위를 따로 염두에 두어야 한다. 무증상 고양이에서도 silent aspiration이 있을 수 있어, 기침 유무만으로 aspiration을 배제하지 말고 영상에서 직접 penetration-aspiration을 확인하는 것이 좋다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
건강한 개 12마리와 고양이 13마리를 대상으로 한 소규모 단일 연구로, 질환 동물에서의 진단 성능은 평가되지 않았다. 품종·체중·급여 방식 등 종 외 교란요인과 검사 프로토콜(조영제 종류·양)에 따른 영향은 초록에 서술되지 않았다. 다중 비교 보정 여부도 초록에 없다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않는 값이어서 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Videofluoroscopic swallow studies (VFSS) are the criterion standard for diagnosing dysphagia in veterinary species. However, a lack of normative swallowing variables in cats hinders assessment of cats with potential aerodigestive disease. Species variation in swallowing could lead to over- and under-interpretation of findings on VFSS, resulting in misdiagnosis.

HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: Compare differences in objective swallowing metrics between cats and dogs using VFSS. We hypothesize that cats will show differences in objective swallowing metrics on VFSS compared with dogs.

ANIMALS: Healthy adult cats (n = 13) and dogs (n = 12) without clinical signs (CS) of respiratory or alimentary disease.

METHODS: Prospective, cross-sectional study. Cats and dogs underwent standardized VFSS. All were evaluated according to 16 objective metrics and compared between species. Between group comparisons were made using Mann-Whitney-U, T-test, or Fischer's exact test with a P < .05 significance level for all comparisons.

RESULTS: Cats had significantly longer inter-swallow intervals, shorter pharyngeal transit times, and more pharyngeal swallows to generate primary peristalsis compared with dogs (P < .05). Cats were also more likely to have esophageal volume accumulation and slowing of distal esophageal transit time compared with dogs (P < .05). Physiologic reflux was identified in 2 (15.4%) cats vs 6 (50%) dogs. Pathologic penetration-aspiration (P-A score: 7; aspiration without attempt to cough) was found in 1 cat despite a lack of respiratory or alimentary CS.

CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Species variation should be considered when interpreting VFSS. In 1 cat, occult tracheal aspiration of contrast (P-A score 7) was detected without accompanying cough.
