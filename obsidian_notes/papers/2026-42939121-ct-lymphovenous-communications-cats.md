# Computed Tomography Findings in Three Cases of Suspected Lymphovenous Communications in Cats.

**Animals (2026)** · 증례군(후향적), n=3 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939121/)
관련 주제: [[topics/vascular-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Contrast-enhanced CT로 의심 spontaneous lymphovenous communication을 보인 고양이 3마리를 후향적으로 기술했다. 세 마리 모두 portal venous tributary에서 시작해 cisterna chyli로 이어지는 dilated, tortuous vessel이 있었고, 기원은 두 마리에서 splenic vein, 한 마리에서 cranial mesenteric vein이었다. Cisterna chyli와 thoracic duct는 일관되게 확장되고 조영증강되었으며, communication 이전(cranial)의 portal vein 직경 감소가 모두에서 있었다. 흉수 또는 복수와 피하 부종이 동반되었고 두 마리에서 chylous pleural effusion이 확인되었다. 저자들은 후천적 portal 또는 lymphatic hemodynamic 이상에 반응한 기존 lymphovenous pathway의 확장·기능 동원일 수 있으나 선천성 가능성도 배제할 수 없다고 했다.

## English summary
In this retrospective case series of three cats, contrast-enhanced CT showed dilated tortuous vessels from portal tributaries (splenic vein in two, cranial mesenteric vein in one) to the cisterna chyli, with dilated enhancing cisterna chyli and thoracic duct and reduced portal vein calibre cranial to the communications. Effusions and subcutaneous edema were present, with chylous pleural effusion in two cats. The authors propose enlargement of pre-existing lymphovenous pathways under acquired portal and/or lymphatic hemodynamic disturbance, without excluding a congenital origin.

## 임상 시사점
Chylous effusion이 있는 고양이 CT에서 anomalous portal vessel이 보이면 cisterna chyli·thoracic duct 확장과 portal vein 직경 변화를 함께 확인하고, 문맥 tributary에서 cisterna chyli로 이어지는 연결 혈관 가능성을 판독 소견에 포함시킬 수 있다. 다만 3예 기술이라 감별 기준이나 치료 방침은 제시할 수 없다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
3마리의 후향적 증례군으로 영상 소견 기술이 중심이며 비교군, 확진 검사(예: 림프관조영, 수술·부검)에 대한 정보는 초록에 없다. 병인(후천성 vs 선천성)과 임상적 의미, 예후는 불확실하다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않는 값이어서 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
Pathological lymphovenous communications are poorly characterized in veterinary medicine, and their computed tomographic features and clinical significance remain unknown. This retrospective case series aimed to describe contrast-enhanced computed tomography findings of suspected spontaneous lymphovenous communications in three cats. All cats had dilated, tortuous vessels arising from portal venous tributaries and extending to the cisterna chyli, originating from the splenic vein in two cats and the cranial mesenteric vein in one. The cisterna chyli and thoracic duct were consistently dilated and contrast-enhancing, and reduced portal vein calibre cranial to the communications was present in all cats. Concurrent findings included pleural or peritoneal effusion and subcutaneous edema, with chylous pleural effusion confirmed in two cats. The recurrent anatomical distribution and associated portal and lymphatic abnormalities suggest that these communications may represent the enlargement and functional recruitment of pre-existing lymphovenous pathways in response to acquired portal and/or lymphatic hemodynamic disturbances, although a congenital origin cannot be excluded. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography may allow the recognition and anatomical characterization of suspected pathological lymphovenous communications in cats and should be considered when anomalous portal vessels are associated with concurrent cisterna chyli and thoracic duct abnormalities.
