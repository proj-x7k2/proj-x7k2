# Clinical and 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging features in a dog with bacterial sepsis of unknown origin.

**The Canadian veterinary journal (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42960181/)
관련 주제: [[topics/nuclear-medicine-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Severe neutropenia와 hypoglycemia로 obtunded 상태였던 12세 개에서 sepsis 의심 하에 18F-FDG PET/CT를 시행했다. 좌측 carpus·tarsus의 patchy FDG avidity(SUVmax 7.18, 5.58), 비장·간의 heterogeneous hypermetabolism(SUVmax 3.42), 양측 신장 hypermetabolism(SUVmax 6.22, FDG excretion 없음), 전 골수강의 diffuse uptake(SUVmax 15.47), 경미한 전신 lymphadenopathy가 확인되었다. 혈액배양으로 sepsis가 확진되었으나 치료에도 악화되어 안락사했고, 부검에서 heart·spleen·brain·liver의 necrotizing vasculitis와 thrombosis 등 septicemia에 합당한 소견이 확인되었다.

## English summary
A 12-year-old dog presented obtunded with severe neutropenia and hypoglycemia. 18F-FDG PET/CT showed FDG avidity in the left carpus and tarsus, spleen, liver, kidneys (without appropriate FDG excretion), diffuse bone marrow uptake (SUVmax 15.47) and mild generalized lymphadenopathy. Blood cultures confirmed sepsis; the dog was euthanized after deterioration, and necropsy showed necrotizing vasculitis with thrombosis consistent with disseminated septicemia. The authors describe this as the first such report with necropsy correlation.

## 임상 시사점
원인 불명 sepsis 환자에서 PET/CT는 전신 감염 병소의 분포를 한 번에 보여줄 수 있으나, 단일 증례이므로 일상적 검사로 권고할 근거는 아니다. 접근 가능한 시설이라면 immunocompromised 환자의 보조 검사로 고려할 수 있는 수준이다.

## 원문 초록
Positron emission tomography with 2-deoxy-2-[fluorine-18] fluoro-D-glucose integrated with computed tomography (18F-FDG PET/CT) imaging identified infection and inflammation earlier and with higher sensitivity than other imaging modalities in human medicine. A small case series explored the use of 18F-FDG PET/CT for detecting the cause of fever of unknown origin in dogs. This report details clinical aspects and 18F-FDG PET/CT imaging in the workup and treatment of a 12-year-old dog that was presented obtunded with severe neutropenia and hypoglycemia secondary to suspected sepsis. The 18F-FDG PET/CT revealed moderate, patchy FDG avidity and soft-tissue swelling of the left carpus and tarsus (SUV max: 7.18 and SUV max: 5.58, respectively), moderate heterogeneous splenic and hepatic hypermetabolic activity (SUV max: 3.42), and bilaterally symmetric renal hypermetabolic activity (SUV max: 6.22) without appropriate flourine-18 fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) excretion. Other notable findings included marked, diffuse 18F-FDG uptake within all bone marrow spaces (SUV max: 15.47) and a generalized, mild lymphadenopathy with moderate hypermetabolic activity. Positive blood cultures confirmed sepsis. Clinical deterioration despite appropriate antibiotic and supportive therapy prompted euthanasia. Necropsy revealed lymphohistiocytic and neutrophilic necrotizing vasculitis with thrombosis and necrosis in heart, spleen, brain, and liver, consistent with septicemia. Enterococcus faecium was isolated from lungs, kidneys, and spleen; Serratia marcescens was isolated from lungs and kidneys. This is the first documented report of the use of 18F-FDG PET/CT for the diagnostic workup of a septic patient in veterinary medicine with corroborative necropsy findings to confirm PET/CT suspicion of disseminated septicemia. Key clinical message: Positron emission tomography with 2-deoxy-2-[fluorine-18] fluoro-D-glucose integrated with computed tomography imaging, which combines the functional, metabolic information from a PET scan with the high-resolution anatomical detail from a CT scan, should be considered as an adjunctive imaging modality for the diagnostic workup to identify focal and systemic infections in veterinary medicine. This is critical for immunocompromised animals in which timely and accurate diagnosis is of utmost importance, either for guiding case management and clinical outcome or for guiding owners through difficult decisions when the prognosis is guarded to poor.
