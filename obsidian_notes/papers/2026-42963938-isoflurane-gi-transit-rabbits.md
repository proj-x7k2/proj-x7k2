# Evaluation of the Effects of Isoflurane Anaesthesia on Gastrointestinal Transit Time, Feed Intake and Defecation in Rabbits.

**Veterinary medicine and science (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=5 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42963938/) · DOI: 10.1002/vms3.71272
관련 주제: [[topics/sedation-restraint-imaging]], [[topics/abdominal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
건강한 성체 수컷 New Zealand White 토끼 5마리를 대상으로, 무마취(대조)와 isoflurane 마취 15분·30분·60분의 4개 조건을 6일 washout을 두고 반복 측정했다. Barium sulphate contrast radiography로 gastric transit time, small intestinal transit time, hard feces 형성 시간을 평가했고, 마취 후 6일간 feed intake와 faecal output도 기록했다. Small intestinal transit time은 조건 간 전반적 차이가 있었으나(Friedman p=0.015) 마취군과 무마취 대조 간 pairwise 비교는 모두 유의하지 않았고, 나머지 지표에는 차이가 없었다.

## English summary
Five healthy male New Zealand White rabbits underwent a repeated-measures study with awake control and 15-, 30- and 60-min isoflurane anaesthesia, separated by 6-day washouts. GI transit was assessed with barium contrast radiography, and feed intake and faecal output were recorded for 6 days. Small intestinal transit time differed overall (p = 0.015), but no anaesthesia group differed significantly from the awake control; gastric transit, time to hard faeces, feed intake and faecal output were unchanged.

## 임상 시사점
최대 60분의 isoflurane 마취가 건강한 토끼의 위장관 통과시간·식욕·분변량에 뚜렷한 영향을 준다는 근거는 이 연구에서 확인되지 않았다. 다만 n=5이므로 "영향 없음"의 증거로 보기는 어렵고, 토끼 CT/MRI 마취 후 GI stasis 모니터링은 기존대로 유지하는 것이 안전하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
건강한 수컷 5마리 repeated-measures 설계로 검정력이 낮고, 저자들도 radiographic 평가 시점이 이산적이라 transit time 측정이 정밀하지 않다고 밝혔다. 질환이 있거나 암컷·고령 토끼, 다른 진정 프로토콜에는 일반화할 수 없다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Domestic rabbits are popular companion animals, resulting in a growing demand for veterinary procedures requiring anaesthesia. However, due to their unique characteristics, anaesthetizing rabbits is challenging and carries a high risk of peri-anaesthetic mortality and gastrointestinal (GI) complications. Isoflurane is a commonly used inhalation anaesthetic, but no studies have assessed its potential effects on GI transit time in rabbits.

OBJECTIVES: The present study aims to assess whether varying durations of isoflurane anaesthesia affect GI transit time, feed intake and defecation in healthy rabbits.

METHODS: Five healthy adult male New Zealand White rabbits participated in a repeated measures study comprising four conditions: (1) awake (control), (2) 15 min, (3) 30 min and (4) 60 min of anaesthesia with isoflurane, each separated by a 6-day washout period. Anaesthesia was induced with 5% isoflurane in 100% oxygen using an induction chamber and maintained with 1.5%-3% isoflurane in oxygen delivered via an endotracheal tube to achieve deep surgical anaesthesia. GI transit time was evaluated via barium sulphate contrast radiography at 1, 15, 30, 60, 120, 240, 360 and 420 min post-administration. The study assessed three transit markers: (1) gastric transit time, (2) small intestinal transit time and (3) time to hard faeces formation. Feed intake and faecal output were also recorded daily for 6 days after each anaesthetic condition.

RESULTS: Gastric transit time was consistent across all experimental conditions, and the contrast agent was seen at T1 minute across all conditions. Small intestinal transit time differed significantly among groups (Friedman test: χ2 (3) = 10.41, p = 0.015, Kendall's W = 0.69); however, pairwise comparisons between anaesthesia conditions and the awake control were not statistically significant. The 60-min group had the highest median transit time (120 min), compared with 60 min in the other three conditions. No significant differences were observed in time to hard faeces formation among conditions (p = 0.152). Feed intake and faecal output did not differ across anaesthetic durations (p > 0.05 for all comparisons).

CONCLUSIONS: Under the experimental conditions studied, isoflurane anaesthesia was not associated with significant changes in gastric transit, the time to hard faeces formation, feed intake or faecal output in healthy male rabbits. Although we observed an overall difference in small intestinal transit time, no anaesthetic group showed a statistically significant variation from the awake control phase. Furthermore, the discrete nature of radiographic assessments and the small sample size limit definitive conclusions regarding a clinically relevant isoflurane-induced transit delay. Further studies with larger sample sizes and more precise transit-time measurement techniques are recommended.
