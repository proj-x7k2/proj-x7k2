# Three-dimensional ultrasonographic assessment of the cranial cruciate ligament in medium- to large-sized canine cadavers: A feasibility study.

**Veterinary journal (London, England : 1997) (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=17 (34 stifle) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42754086/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.tvjl.2026.106890
관련 주제: [[topics/musculoskeletal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Stifle 질환 병력이 없는 중형~대형견 사체 17구(34개 stifle)를 대상으로 3D 초음파(3D-US)를 이용한 cranial cruciate ligament(CrCL) 시각화·탐색적 계측의 feasibility를 평가했다. 모든 stifle에서 2D-US에 이어 3D-US로 CrCL의 femoral·tibial attachment와 중간 인대 부분을 식별할 수 있었고, 근위·중위·원위부 직경을 탐색적으로 측정했다. Exploratory lateral arthrotomy로 확인한 결과 모든 인대는 정상이었으며 초음파 소견과 일치했다. 저자들은 3D-US가 중형~대형견 사체에서 CrCL 평가에 실현 가능함을 보여주었으나, 생체 개에서의 임상 적용 가능성·재현성·진단능은 추가 연구가 필요하다고 밝혔다.

## English summary
This observational descriptive feasibility study examined 17 canine cadavers (34 stifle joints) with no history of stifle disease using both 2D and 3D ultrasonography (3D-US) to visualize and exploratorily measure the cranial cruciate ligament (CrCL), followed by exploratory lateral arthrotomy to confirm ligament integrity. Using 3D-US, the femoral and tibial CrCL attachments and intervening ligament portions were identified in all examined joints, with exploratory diameter measurements obtained at proximal, middle, and distal regions; no ligament abnormalities were detected, consistent with arthrotomy findings. The authors conclude 3D-US is feasible for CrCL assessment in medium- to large-sized canine cadavers, but clinical applicability, repeatability, and diagnostic performance in live dogs with CrCL disease require further study.

## 원문 초록
Cranial cruciate ligament (CrCL) rupture is a common cause of stifle instability and pelvic limb lameness in dogs. Although complete ruptures are often suspected based on clinical examination, assessment of the CrCL remains challenging using non-invasive conventional imaging modalities. The aim of this study was to evaluate the feasibility of three-dimensional ultrasonography (3D-US) for visualization and exploratory measurement of the CrCL in medium- to large-sized canine cadavers. An observational descriptive study was conducted on 17 adult dog cadavers (34 stifle joints) with no history of stifle disease and no conventional two-dimensional ultrasonographic (2D-US) evidence of stifle pathological changes. Each stifle joint was examined using both 2D-US and 3D-US, followed by exploratory lateral arthrotomy to confirm ligament integrity. Using 3D-US, the femoral and tibial attachments of the CrCL, together with intervening ligament portions, were identified in all examined stifle joints. Exploratory diameter measurements were obtained at the proximal, middle and distal regions of all examined CrCLs. No ligament abnormalities were detected on ultrasonographic examination and ligament integrity was subsequently confirmed by arthrotomy. These findings demonstrate the feasibility of 3D-US for assessment of the CrCL in medium- to large-sized canine cadavers. Further studies are required to determine the clinical applicability, repeatability and diagnostic performance of this technique in live dogs with CrCL disease.
