# Whippets have larger abdominal caudal vena cava to aorta ratios than other dogs on computed tomography.

**Frontiers in Veterinary Science (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=68 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42997290/)
관련 주제: [[topics/cardiac-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
심혈관 질환이 알려지지 않은 개 68마리(whippet 34, 비-whippet 대조 34)의 CT에서 abdominal과 thoracic 표준 위치의 caudal vena cava to aorta ratio(CVC/Ao)를 후향적으로 비교했다. Whippet의 abdominal CVC/Ao가 대조군보다 유의하게 컸고(1.54±0.29 vs 1.28±0.23; p<0.001) 체중·연령 보정 후에도 유지되었으나(보정 차이 0.29, 95% CI 0.15-0.42), thoracic ratio는 차이가 없었다(1.22 vs 1.28; p=0.29). 두 군 모두 기존 발표된 CT 기준값을 초과했고, 초음파 상한을 넘는 비율은 whippet이 더 높았다(73.5% vs 41.2%; p=0.007).

## English summary
In 68 dogs without known cardiovascular disease (34 whippets, 34 controls), whippets had a higher abdominal CVC/Ao on CT (1.54 vs 1.28, p<0.001, persisting after adjustment for weight and age) but similar thoracic ratios. Thoracic ratio declined with age (-0.023 per year). Both groups exceeded published CT reference values, and more whippets exceeded the ultrasonographic upper limit (73.5% vs 41.2%). Observed mean ± 2 SD limits are cohort observations, not breed-specific reference ranges.

## 임상 시사점
CT로 CVC/Ao를 volume status 지표로 해석할 때, 마취 하 CT 값에 초음파나 기존 CT 기준값을 그대로 적용하지 말아야 하며, 특히 whippet 같은 sighthound의 abdominal ratio가 높게 나와도 곧바로 volume overload로 해석하지 않는 것이 안전하다. 사용하는 측정 위치(abdominal vs thoracic)를 기록해 둘 것.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 연구 후향적 비교(n=68)로 마취 조건, 수액·조영 타이밍 등 CT 촬영 조건의 영향은 초록에 상세히 없다. 제시된 mean ± 2 SD 범위는 저자들도 breed-specific reference range가 아니라 예비 관찰값이라고 밝혔다. 건강 상태는 "알려진 심혈관 질환 없음"에 근거한다.

## 원문 초록
INTRODUCTION: Whippets are sighthounds with distinct cardiovascular adaptations associated with athletic performance. The caudal vena cava to aorta ratio (CVC/Ao) is an established index of intravascular volume status, assessed ultrasonographically and on computed tomography (CT) at thoracic and abdominal sites, but breed-specific differences remain unreported. We compared thoracic and abdominal CVC/Ao in whippets and non-whippet controls, and with published reference values, and hypothesised that whippets would have higher ratios.

METHODS: Sixty-eight dogs without known cardiovascular disease (34 whippets, 34 non-whippet controls) were retrospectively reviewed. CTs were performed under general anaesthesia, with CVC/Ao measured at standardised abdominal and thoracic sites. Groups were compared with Welch's two-sample t-tests, sites within animals with paired t-tests, and analysis of covariance adjusted for weight and age. Significance was set at p < 0.05.

RESULTS: Whippets had significantly higher abdominal CVC/Ao than controls (1.54 ± 0.29 vs. 1.28 ± 0.23; p < 0.001), whereas thoracic ratios did not differ (1.22 ± 0.23 vs. 1.28 ± 0.26; p = 0.29). The abdominal difference persisted after adjustment for weight and age (adjusted difference 0.29, 95% CI 0.15-0.42; p < 0.001). Within whippets, abdominal ratios exceeded thoracic ratios (p < 0.001), a difference not seen in controls (p = 0.99). The thoracic ratio declined with age independently of breed and weight (-0.023 per year; p = 0.002). Both groups exceeded published CT values at both sites, and a greater proportion of whippets than controls exceeded the ultrasonographic upper limit (73.5% vs. 41.2%; p = 0.007). Observed mean ± 2 SD limits were 0.96-2.13 (abdomen, whippets), 0.82-1.75 (abdomen, controls) and 0.76-1.74 (thorax, combined); these are cohort observations rather than breed-specific reference ranges.

DISCUSSION: Whippets had larger abdominal CVC/Ao than controls, with comparable thoracic ratios, indicating a site-specific increase not attributable to body size or age. Because both groups exceeded published values, reference values are not transferable across imaging modality irrespective of breed or anaesthetic status. Modality-, breed- and possibly age-specific ranges are needed, and we provide preliminary limits as a starting point.
