# Case Report: Ventriculoperitoneal shunt for management of syringomyelia and Chiari-like malformation in a Cavalier King Charles Spaniel.

**Frontiers in Veterinary Science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42877668/) · DOI: 10.7759/cureus.18392
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]], [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
spinal hyperesthesia, 운동 거부, phantom scratching을 보인 1세 수컷 Cavalier King Charles Spaniel(CKCS)의 증례다. MRI에서 Chiari-like malformation(CM), brachycephaly, cerebellar herniation, ventriculomegaly, odontoid peg부터 L5까지 이어지는 syringomyelia(C2-C3 최대 횡폭 7.0mm)가 확인되었다. meloxicam·gabapentin에 반응하지 않고 lateral ventricle 확장과 syrinx 진행이 우려되어 ventriculoperitoneal shunt(VPS)를 시행했다. 일과성 postoperative pneumocephalus 외에 합병증은 없었고, 추적 MRI에서 syrinx 감소와 lateral ventricle 정상화가 확인되었으며 수술 6개월 후 syrinx는 2.3mm였다.

## English summary
A 1-year-old male CKCS with spinal hyperesthesia and phantom scratching had MRI evidence of Chiari-like malformation, cerebellar herniation, ventriculomegaly and syringomyelia from the odontoid peg to L5 (maximum width 7.0 mm at C2-C3). After medical management failed, a ventriculoperitoneal shunt was placed. Follow-up MRI showed syrinx reduction and resolution of ventriculomegaly (syrinx 2.3 mm at 6 months); the dog remained pain-free, with a brief suspected movement disorder episode at 8 months.

## 임상 시사점
CM/SM 개에서 ventriculomegaly가 뚜렷하면 syrinx 폭뿐 아니라 lateral ventricle 크기도 함께 평가·추적 대상에 포함할 만하다. 다만 단일 증례이므로 VPS 적응증을 정하는 근거로는 부족하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로 대조군이 없고 추적 기간이 제한적이다. syrinx 감소가 shunt 효과인지 자연 경과인지 구분할 수 없다.

## 원문 초록
A 1-year-old male Cavalier King Charles Spaniel (CKCS) was presented with spinal hyperesthesia, reluctance to exercise, and phantom scratching. MRI revealed a Chiari-like malformation (CM) with brachycephaly, cerebellar herniation, ventriculomegaly, and syringomyelia extending from the odontoid peg to L5, with a maximum transverse syrinx width of 7.0 mm at C2-C3. Medical management (meloxicam and gabapentin) failed to resolve clinical signs. Due to significantly increased lateral ventricles and concern for syrinx progression, a ventriculoperitoneal shunt was placed. Aside from transient post-operative pneumocephalus, the procedure was successful. The dog showed significant clinical improvement, and follow-up MRI demonstrated syrinx reduction and resolution of lateral ventricles. At 6 months post-op, the syrinx measured 2.3 mm. Eight months after the surgery, the dog experienced a brief episode of a suspected movement disorder like but recovered fully. The patient remains pain-free and neurologically normal. This case describes the clinical and imaging outcomes of ventriculoperitoneal shunting (VPS) in a dog with Chiari-like malformation, syringomyelia, and ventriculomegaly.
