# Effects of recumbency and tube current on image quality of canine elbow computed tomography.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=20 (사체, 40 elbow) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42761083/) · DOI: 10.1111/vru.12823
관련 주제: [[topics/musculoskeletal-imaging]] · [[topics/imaging-technique-safety]]

## 국문 요약
개 사체 20구(elbow 40개)를 대상으로 dorsal recumbency(머리 중립위, 양쪽 elbow 동시 촬영)와 lateral recumbency(머리·목 미측 굴곡, elbow 개별 촬영) 두 CT 자세 프로토콜과 tube current(150mA vs 300mA)가 elbow CT 영상 품질에 미치는 영향을 비교했다. Lateral recumbency는 150mA에서 4가지 정량 지표(bone·soft-tissue kernel의 signal-to-noise, contrast-to-noise ratio) 모두에서 dorsal recumbency보다 유의하게 우수했고(p<0.001), 150mA lateral이 300mA dorsal보다도 우수했다(p=0.002~0.013). Tube current를 150mA에서 300mA로 올려도 동일 자세 내에서는 유의한 개선이 없었으며(p≥0.058), 판독자들은 artifact·연부조직 대비에서 lateral recumbency를 99~100% 선호했으나 bone conspicuity에서는 선호도가 엇갈렸다(비-동률 중 60.6%가 lateral 선호, p=0.008). 저자들은 medial coronoid process 평가를 위한 일상적 정형외과 CT에는 dorsal recumbency도 합리적 선택이지만, artifact 감소나 연부조직 평가가 중요할 때는 lateral recumbency를 권장했다.

## English summary
In this prospective experimental cadaver study of 20 canine cadavers (40 elbows), two CT positioning protocols (dorsal recumbency with both elbows scanned together vs. lateral recumbency with elbows scanned separately) and tube current (150 vs 300 mA) were compared for elbow CT image quality. Lateral recumbency produced significantly higher signal- and contrast-to-noise ratios than dorsal recumbency across all four outcomes at 150 mA (p<0.001), and lateral at 150 mA outperformed dorsal at 300 mA (p=0.002-0.013). Doubling tube current from 150 to 300 mA did not significantly improve quality within the same recumbency (p≥0.058). Observers almost unanimously preferred lateral recumbency for artifact and soft-tissue criteria (99-100%), though preference for bone conspicuity was less consistent. The authors conclude dorsal recumbency remains reasonable for routine medial coronoid process CT, while lateral recumbency should be preferred when artifact reduction or soft-tissue evaluation matters.

## 임상 시사점
Elbow dysplasia 스크리닝 목적의 일상적 CT는 dorsal recumbency로 양측을 함께 촬영해도 무방하지만, artifact가 문제되거나 연부조직 평가가 중요한 증례에서는 lateral recumbency로 개별 촬영하는 것이 영상 품질 면에서 더 유리하며, tube current를 단순히 올리는 것은 자세 최적화를 대체하지 못한다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
개 사체 20구(40 elbow)를 이용한 전향적 실험 연구로, 정량 지표(SNR/CNR)와 3인 판독자의 정성 선호도 평가를 함께 사용했다. 사체 연구이므로 생체 내 호흡·근긴장 등에 의한 영상 품질 차이는 반영되지 않았고, medial coronoid process 병변 자체의 진단 정확도(민감도·특이도) 비교가 아니라 영상 품질 지표 비교에 초점을 맞춘 연구라는 점에 유의해야 한다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Elbow dysplasia is a leading cause of forelimb lameness in medium- to large-breed dogs, and computed tomography (CT) is the imaging modality of choice for assessment of the medial coronoid process. Image quality depends on patient positioning and tube current, but their effects have not been systematically compared.

AIMS: To compare the image quality of two canine elbow CT positioning protocols (dorsal recumbency: head in neutral position, both elbows scanned together vs. lateral recumbency: head/neck flexed caudally, elbows scanned separately), and to evaluate the influence of tube current and its interaction with these protocols.

METHODS: Prospective experimental cadaver study on twenty canine cadavers (40 elbows). All cadavers were scanned in both positions at 150 mA; a subgroup of eight was additionally scanned at 300 mA. Quantitative outcomes were signal- and contrast-to-noise ratios in bone- and soft-tissue-kernels. Qualitative outcomes, assessed by three observers, were paired-preference reads of four criteria: bone conspicuity, soft-tissue conspicuity, and artifacts in bone- and soft-tissue window. Inter- and intra-observer agreement were quantified for the bone conspicuity criterion.

RESULTS: Lateral recumbency produced significantly higher signal- and contrast-to-noise ratios than dorsal recumbency for all four outcomes (all p < 0.001 at 150 mA). Doubling the tube current from 150 to 300 mA did not produce a statistically significant within-recumbency improvement (all p ≥ 0.058). Lateral recumbency at 150 mA remained superior to dorsal at 300 mA on all four outcomes (p = 0.002 to 0.013). Qualitatively, observers almost unanimously preferred lateral recumbency for the artifact criteria and for soft-tissue conspicuity (99-100% lateral). For bone conspicuity, 31% of paired comparisons were ties; lateral was preferred in 60.6% of non-tie ratings (p = 0.008).

CONCLUSION: Lateral recumbency, scanning the elbows separately with head and neck flexed caudally, provides superior objective image quality, and increased tube current does not compensate for suboptimal positioning. The observer-perceived advantage for bone conspicuity was inconsistent. For routine orthopedic CT focused on the medial coronoid process, dorsal recumbency remains a reasonable choice; lateral recumbency should be preferred when artifact reduction or soft-tissue evaluation is clinically important.
