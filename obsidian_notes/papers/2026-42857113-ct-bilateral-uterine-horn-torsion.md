# Case Report: Computed tomographic diagnosis of asymmetric bilateral uterine horn torsion secondary to benign uterine leiomyomas in a non-gravid dog.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42857113/) · DOI: 10.2460/javma.248.6.661
관련 주제: [[topics/urogenital-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
11세 미중성화 암컷 Pomeranian이 2~3일간의 복부팽만·호흡곤란·식욕부진·구토·oliguria로 내원해, 신장 손상·전해질 이상·전신 염증 소견이 확인된 증례보고다. 방사선·초음파에서 양측 복부 종괴와 복막삼출이 확인되었으나 기원 장기는 특정할 수 없었다. 조영증강 CT에서 양측 자궁각에서 기원한 종괴가 확인되었으며, 우측은 중심부 조영증강이 없고 좌측은 미약한 중심부 조영증강이 있으면서 두 종괴 모두 얇은 말초 rim enhancement를 보여 vascular compromise와 ischemic injury를 시사했다. 양쪽 난소는 조영증강이 보존되었고, 양측 자궁각의 spiral twisting과 whirl sign이(우측에서 더 뚜렷) 확인되어 수술 전 asymmetric bilateral uterine horn torsion으로 진단했다. 개복술에서 우측 각의 약 1,080° 회전(좌측 각 주위)과 좌측 각 자체축 270° 회전이 확인되었고, broad ligament 육안적 파열은 없었다. En bloc ovariohysterectomy와 vaginal mass 절제를 시행했고, 조직검사에서 양측 자궁각·vaginal mass 모두 leiomyoma로 확진되었다. 개는 수술 후 9개월간 임상적으로 양호했다.

## English summary
This case report describes an 11-year-old intact female Pomeranian presenting with a 2- to 3-day history of abdominal distension, dyspnea, anorexia, vomiting, and oliguria, with laboratory findings indicating renal compromise, electrolyte abnormalities, and systemic inflammation. Radiography and ultrasonography revealed large bilateral abdominal masses with peritoneal effusion, but the organ of origin could not be determined. Contrast-enhanced CT demonstrated masses arising from both uterine horns, with absent central enhancement on the right, minimal central enhancement on the left, and thin peripheral rim enhancement on both, suggesting vascular compromise and ischemic injury; both ovaries showed preserved enhancement. Spiral twisting of both uterine horns and bilateral whirl signs, more pronounced on the right, allowed a preoperative diagnosis of asymmetric bilateral uterine horn torsion. Exploratory laparotomy confirmed approximately 1,080° rotation of the right horn around the left horn and 270° rotation of the left horn around its own axis, without gross rupture of the broad ligament. En bloc ovariohysterectomy and excision of a vaginal mass were performed, with histopathology confirming leiomyomas of both uterine horns and the vaginal mass. The dog remained clinically well 9 months after surgery.

## 임상 시사점
암컷 개에서 원인 불명의 양측 복부 종괴·복막삼출이 있을 때, 방사선·초음파로 기원 장기를 특정할 수 없다면 contrast-enhanced CT로 whirl sign과 peripheral rim enhancement 패턴을 확인하는 것이 uterine horn torsion(특히 양측성·비대칭성)의 수술 전 진단에 도움이 될 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로, 양측 비대칭 uterine horn torsion의 CT 소견(특히 bilateral whirl sign)이 다른 복강 내 종괴·torsion 질환과 어떻게 감별되는지, 이 소견의 민감도·특이도는 평가할 수 없다. Leiomyoma가 torsion의 선행 원인이었는지 결과였는지도 단일 증례로는 명확히 구분하기 어렵다.

## 원문 초록
Uterine torsion is uncommon in dogs and most commonly involves a single uterine horn. An 11-year-old intact female Pomeranian was presented with a 2- to 3-day history of abdominal distension, dyspnea, anorexia, vomiting, and oliguria. Clinicopathologic findings indicated renal compromise, electrolyte abnormalities, tissue injury, and marked systemic inflammation. Radiography and ultrasonography revealed large bilateral abdominal masses with peritoneal effusion, although the organ of origin could not be determined. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) demonstrated masses arising from both uterine horns, with absent central enhancement on the right, minimal central enhancement on the left, and thin peripheral rim enhancement of both masses, suggesting vascular compromise and ischemic injury. Both ovaries showed preserved contrast enhancement, and spiral twisting of both uterine horns and bilateral whirl signs were identified, with the changes being markedly more pronounced on the right. These findings permitted a preoperative diagnosis of asymmetric bilateral uterine horn torsion. Exploratory laparotomy confirmed approximately 1,080° rotation of the right horn around the left horn and 270° rotation of the left horn around its own axis, without gross rupture of the broad ligament. En bloc ovariohysterectomy without prior detorsion and excision of the vaginal mass were performed. Histopathologic examination confirmed leiomyomas of both uterine horns and of the vaginal mass, with extensive hemorrhage and necrosis of the right horn. The dog recovered uneventfully and remained clinically well 9 months after surgery. To the authors' knowledge, this is the first report of bilateral uterine torsion associated with benign leiomyomas in a dog and the first description of bilateral whirl signs on CT in canine uterine torsion.
