# Multimodal Postmortem Investigation of Fatal Thoracic Metallic Projectile Trauma in a Jindo Dog: Radiographic, Computed Tomographic, Necropsy, and Histopathological Findings.

**Animals (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42939195/)
관련 주제: [[topics/trauma-forensic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
South Korea에서 경찰이 의뢰한 성체 수컷 Jindo dog의 치명적 penetrating trauma를 사후에 평가했다. Digital radiography에서 좌측 흉부의 다발성 radiopaque metallic opacity, 중증 pleural effusion, subcutaneous emphysema가 보였다. Postmortem CT(16-slice MDCT, 0.75mm)는 좌측 흉벽의 clustered metallic fragment, 좌측 제4 늑골의 oblique displaced fracture, hemopneumothorax와 compression atelectasis, pneumomediastinum을 보였고 두개·복부 손상은 없었다. 부검에서 약 100 mL 흉강 혈액과 양측 cranial lung lobe의 through-and-through laceration, 금속 파편이 확인되었고 조직검사는 생존 중 손상(survival interval <1시간)을 뒷받침했다. 사인은 acute hemopneumothorax와 양측 폐 laceration이다.

## English summary
In a forensic case of a Jindo dog with fatal penetrating thoracic trauma, radiography showed multiple metallic opacities, pleural effusion and subcutaneous emphysema in the left thorax. Postmortem CT delineated clustered metallic fragments, a displaced left fourth rib fracture, hemopneumothorax with compression atelectasis and pneumomediastinum, and excluded cranial or abdominal injury. Necropsy and histopathology confirmed bilateral pulmonary laceration with antemortem vital reaction; the authors conclude that integrating radiography, PMCT, necropsy and histopathology is valuable in medicolegal investigations.

## 임상 시사점
총상·projectile 의심 사례에서는 radiography로 이물 위치를 확인한 뒤 CT로 파편 분포, 골절, 기흉·혈흉을 평가하고 다른 체강 손상을 배제하면 부검 전 계획을 세우는 데 도움이 된다. 증거 가치를 위해 영상 촬영 시 chain-of-custody를 고려해야 한다.

## 원문 초록
Metallic projectile injuries in companion animals present complex diagnostic and evidentiary challenges, especially when comprehensive crime scene data and intact projectiles are absent. This report describes the forensic postmortem investigation of an adult male Jindo dog submitted by law enforcement authorities following fatal penetrating trauma in South Korea. Digital radiography identified multiple radiopaque metallic opacities in the left thoracic region, accompanied by severe pleural effusion and subcutaneous emphysema. Postmortem computed tomography (PMCT; 16-slice MDCT, 0.75-mm slice thickness) precisely delineated clustered hyperattenuating metallic fragments embedded in the left thoracic wall, an oblique displaced fracture of the left fourth rib, marked hemopneumothorax with compression atelectasis, and pneumomediastinum, while excluding cranial or abdominal injury. At necropsy, approximately 100 mL of pleural blood was evacuated, and through-and-through pulmonary transcapsular lacerations were identified traversing the right and left cranial lung lobes. Multiple deformed metallic fragments were recovered adjacent to the fractured rib under strict forensic chain-of-custody protocols. Histopathology of pulmonary laceration margins confirmed acute hemorrhage, fibrin deposition, and early neutrophilic margination, establishing an acute antemortem vital reaction (survival interval < 1 h). Histological examination of the cutaneous entry and exit margins revealed mechanical tearing without thermal coagulation or soot residue, consistent with an intermediate-to-distant discharge. The anatomical convergence of lesions supported a right-to-left projectile trajectory, with the left axillary defect presenting features compatible with a shored exit wound. Death was attributed to fatal acute hemopneumothorax and bilateral pulmonary laceration secondary to perforating metallic projectile injury. This case demonstrates the indispensable diagnostic and prosecutorial value of integrating digital radiography, PMCT virtopsy, forensic necropsy, and histopathology in veterinary medicolegal investigations of non-accidental trauma.
