# Fluorescein sodium-guided resection of cervical peripheral nerve sheath tumors in 3 dogs.

**Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association (2026)** · 증례군, n=3 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025055/) · DOI: 10.2460/javma.26.08.0620
관련 주제: [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
경추 peripheral nerve sheath tumor(PNST) 개 3마리(10-11세, 4.2-5.4kg)를 대상으로 fluorescein sodium(20 mg/kg IV)을 수술 노출 후 투여해 절제를 유도한 증례군이다. 술전 MRI에서 extraforaminal extension을 동반한 contrast-enhancing intradural extramedullary mass가 확인되었다. 종양 조직은 일반 조명에서 노란색으로 보였고 395-nm UV 조명에서 2마리가 bright fluorescence를 보였으며, 재수술 2예에서는 술후·방사선 유발 fibrosis와 시각적으로 구분되었다. 3마리 모두 gross total resection을 달성했고 fluorescein 관련 부작용은 없었으나, MPNST 2마리는 4개월 내 원격 또는 전이성 질환이 발생했다.

## English summary
Three dogs with cervical PNST underwent fluorescein sodium (20 mg/kg IV)-guided resection after preoperative MRI showed contrast-enhancing intradural extramedullary masses with extraforaminal extension. Tumor tissue appeared yellow under standard light and fluoresced under 395-nm UV in 2 dogs, and in 2 re-operative cases was distinguishable from postoperative and radiation-induced fibrosis. Gross total resection was achieved in all, with no fluorescein-related adverse events. Both MPNST dogs developed remote or metastatic disease within 4 months; the schwannoma dog was recurrence-free at 3 months.

## 임상 시사점
경추 PNST 수술에서 술전 MRI로 범위를 평가한 뒤, 특히 재수술처럼 fibrosis와 종양의 구분이 어려운 상황에서 fluorescein이 보조 시각 정보가 될 수 있다. 다만 nonfluorescent tissue는 생검하지 않았으므로 민감도·특이도를 알 수 없고, 수술자의 판단을 대체하지 못한다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
개 3마리의 증례군으로 비교군이 없고, 형광 소견과 병리 결과의 일치도(nonfluorescent tissue 생검 없음)는 평가되지 않았다. 추적 기간이 3-4개월로 짧아 절제 범위가 재발·생존에 미치는 영향은 알 수 없다. 영상 측면은 술전 MRI 기술이 중심이며, 구체적인 MRI 수치는 초록에 없다.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVE: To describe the intraoperative findings and outcomes of fluorescein sodium-guided resection of cervical peripheral nerve sheath tumors (PNSTs) in dogs, including in the re-operative setting.

ANIMALS: 3 client-owned dogs with cervical PNST. Two dogs underwent a second resection of progressive malignant PNST (MPNST) after subtotal resection and stereotactic radiation therapy performed elsewhere. One dog underwent primary resection of a previously untreated PNST identified as a schwannoma.

CLINICAL PRESENTATION: Affected dogs were between 10 and 11 years of age (weighing between 4.2 and 5.4 kg) and presented with cervical pain, hyperesthesia, or tetraparesis. Preoperative MRI revealed contrast-enhancing intradural extramedullary masses with extraforaminal extensions.

RESULTS: Fluorescein sodium (20 mg/kg) was administered IV after surgical exposure. Tumor tissue exhibited yellow discoloration under standard illumination in all dogs and bright fluorescence under 395-nm UV illumination in 2 dogs and, in the 2 re-operative cases, was visually distinct from the surrounding postoperative and radiation-induced fibrosis, which was grossly assessed as nondiseased and did not take up fluorescein. This contrast assisted dissection, and gross total resection was achieved in all dogs; nonfluorescent tissue was not biopsied. Histopathology confirmed MPNST in 2 dogs and schwannoma in 1 dog. Fluorescein-related adverse events were not observed. Both MPNST cases developed remote or metastatic disease within 4 months (1 euthanized); the schwannoma case was recurrence-free at 3 months.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Fluorescein sodium-guided resection of canine cervical PNSTs is feasible with widely available equipment and may aid intraoperative identification of tumor tissue in both primary and re-operative settings, suggesting a lower equipment barrier than commonly assumed. Because nonfluorescent tissue was not sampled, fluorescein should be an adjunct to, not a substitute for, surgical judgment.
