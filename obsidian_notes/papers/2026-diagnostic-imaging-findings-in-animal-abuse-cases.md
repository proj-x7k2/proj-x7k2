## 국문 요약
비엔나 수의과대학에서 2014-2023년 동안 동물 학대 의심 사례 11,689건 중 83건을 선별하여 영상진단 기록을 후향적으로 분석했다. 개, 고양이, 조류, 토끼목, 파충류에서 골절과 총상이 가장 흔한 손상 소견이었으며, 진단에는 방사선촬영과 CT가 주로 사용되고 초음파나 MRI는 전혀 활용되지 않았다. 조류와 파충류처럼 해부학적 구조가 복잡한 종에서는 CT가 특히 유용했고, 방사선촬영은 전 종에서 효과적이었다. 저자들은 영상진단이 학대 사례에서 객관적 증거를 제공하여 임상적 평가와 법적 절차를 뒷받침한다고 결론지었다.

## English summary
This retrospective study reviewed imaging records from 11,689 companion and exotic animals at the University of Veterinary Medicine Vienna (2014-2023) and identified 83 cases of suspected animal abuse. Skeletal fractures and ballistic trauma were the most common findings across dogs, cats, birds, lagomorphs, and reptiles, with radiography and CT as the primary modalities used; ultrasound and MRI were not used for case qualification. CT was particularly valuable in birds and reptiles due to their anatomical complexity, while radiography was effective across all species. The findings support diagnostic imaging as objective evidence for forensic veterinary investigations and legal proceedings.

## 원문 초록
Animal abuse is an increasingly recognized societal concern, with growing emphasis on the role of veterinary teams in the identification and documentation of suspected abuse. Diagnostic imaging is pivotal in identifying hidden injuries, characterizing trauma patterns, and supporting medical and legal decision-making. This retrospective study analyzed diagnostic imaging in suspected animal abuse cases over a decade at the University of Veterinary Medicine Vienna (Vetmeduni Vienna). Between 2014 and 2023, records and imaging archives of 11,689 companion and exotic animals, including cats, dogs, birds, lagomorphs, and reptiles, were reviewed. Large animals were excluded from this study. Cases were selected based on key abuse-related terms (e.g., animal abuse, gunshot wound, pellet, projectile) and the use of imaging modalities: radiography (x-ray), computed tomography (CT), ultrasonography (US), or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI). 83 cases were identified as suspected abuse, including 16 deceased animals, 14 of which underwent necropsy. Clinical and diagnostic histories were analyzed for all cases. Across species, predominant injuries included skeletal fractures and ballistic trauma, often multiple or combined. No cases utilized US or MRI for case qualification. Across all species, imaging revealed high rates of trauma, including fractures (common in dogs, cats, and reptiles) and ballistic injuries (most frequent in birds and dogs). CT was especially valuable in birds and reptiles due to anatomical complexity, while radiography was effective across all species. These findings demonstrate that diagnostic imaging provides objective, case-specific evidence that can guide clinical evaluation, document internal injuries, and support forensic investigations and legal proceedings.
