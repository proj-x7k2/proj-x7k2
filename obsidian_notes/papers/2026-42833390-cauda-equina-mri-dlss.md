# MRI FINDINGS IN CAUDA EQUINA NERVE ROOTS AND ASSOCIATED CLINICAL SIGNS IN DOGS WITH DEGENERATIVE LUMBOSACRAL STENOSIS.

**Veterinary journal (London, England : 1997) (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=67 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42833390/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.tvjl.2026.106916
관련 주제: [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Degenerative lumbosacral stenosis(DLSS) 개 50마리와 대조군 17마리(총 67마리)를 대상으로 high-field MRI에서 cauda equina nerve root의 크기, 신호 특성, 위치(nerve root sedimentation sign, NRSS), 주행(redundant nerve roots, RNR)을 평가하고 임상 증상과의 연관성을 분석한 후향적 case-control 연구다. Lumbosacral foraminal stenosis는 DLSS군 66%, 대조군 5.88%에서 확인되었고(p<0.001), lumbar nerve root pathology는 DLSS군 60%, 대조군 11.77%에서 확인되었다(p<0.001). 편측 임상 증상은 동측 foraminal stenosis와 유의하게 연관되었고, L7 nerve root 비후도 동측 신경 기능장애와 유의하게 연관되었다. NRSS는 양군 간 차이가 없었으나 RNR은 DLSS와 약한~중등도 연관성을 보였다.

## English summary
This retrospective, blinded, case-control study evaluated the size, signal characteristics, position (nerve root sedimentation sign, NRSS), and course (redundant nerve roots, RNR) of cauda equina nerve roots using high-field MRI in 50 dogs with degenerative lumbosacral stenosis (DLSS) and 17 control dogs, and assessed associations with clinical signs. Lumbosacral foraminal stenosis was found in 66% of DLSS cases versus 5.88% of controls (p < 0.001), and lumbar nerve root pathology in 60% versus 11.77% (p < 0.001). Unilateral clinical signs were significantly associated with ipsilateral foraminal stenosis, and ipsilateral L7 nerve root thickening was significantly associated with unilateral nerve root dysfunction. NRSS did not differ between groups, while RNR showed a small-to-moderate association with DLSS.

## 임상 시사점
DLSS가 의심되는 개의 MRI를 판독할 때 central canal·intervertebral foramen 협착 외에도 nerve root 비후(특히 L7)와 foraminal stenosis의 좌우 대칭성을 함께 평가하면, 편측 임상 증상의 해부학적 원인을 더 구체적으로 짚어낼 수 있다. NRSS는 진단적 가치가 낮아 보조 지표로서의 비중을 낮추는 것이 합리적이다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
DLSS로 진단된 client-owned 개 50마리와 임상 증상이 없는 대조군 17마리를 대상으로 한 후향적, 블라인드 case-control 연구로, high-field MRI 소견과 임상 증상의 연관성을 통계적으로 분석했다. 대조군 수가 DLSS군보다 적고 단일기관 자료이며, MRI 소견과 임상 증상 간 연관성 분석은 인과관계를 증명하지 않는다는 한계가 있다.

## 원문 초록
Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is the most commonly used advanced imaging modality for diagnosing degenerative lumbosacral stenosis (DLSS). However, stenosis of the central canal and intervertebral foramina does not reliably correlate with clinical signs, and additional diagnostic imaging criteria would strengthen the validity of MRI. This study aimed to evaluate the size, signal characteristics, position (nerve root sedimentation sign, NRSS), and course (redundant nerve roots, RNR) of cauda equina nerve roots in dogs with and without DLSS using high-field MRI, and to assess associations between these findings and clinical signs. A retrospective, blinded, case-control study was performed in client-owned dogs, including 50 dogs diagnosed with DLSS and 17 control dogs without clinical signs of DLSS. Lumbosacral foraminal stenosis was identified in 33/50 (66%) DLSS cases and 1/17 (5.88%) controls (p < 0.001, φ = 0.56). Lumbar nerve root pathology was observed in 30/50 (60%) cases and 2/17 (11.77%) controls (p < 0.001, φ = 0.42). A significant association was found between unilateral clinical signs and ipsilateral foraminal stenosis (p = 0.014, φ = 0.40 for the left; p = 0.018, φ = 0.43 for the right side). Ipsilateral thickening of the L7 nerve root was also significantly associated with unilateral nerve root dysfunction (p = 0.033, φ = 0.35 for the left; p = 0.01, φ = 0.45 for the right side). The NRSS findings did not differ significantly between case and control dogs (p = 0.237, φ = 0.15), whereas RNR showed a small to moderate association with DLSS (p < 0.028, φ = 0.27). While NRSS and RNR were not clearly associated with clinical signs in dogs with DLSS, thickening of the L7 nerve root showed significant correlation with clinical dysfunction. The presence of increased nerve root size may enhance the diagnostic utility of high-field MRI in dogs with DLSS.
