# Suspected recurrent sarcoma resembling feline injection-site sarcoma with spinal cord invasion in a cat.

**JFMS open reports (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42724435/) · DOI: 10.5070/D328157063
관련 주제: [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
6세 중성화 수컷 domestic shorthair가 feline injection-site sarcoma(FISS) 의심 병변 절제 4개월 후 우측 caudodorsolateral hip의 재발성 피하 종괴로 내원했다. Advanced imaging에서 sciatic nerve·직장에 밀접하게 인접한 bilobed soft tissue mass가 확인되었다. 수술적 절제는 주변 구조로 인해 제한되었고 조직검사에서 불완전 절제연을 가진 sarcoma가 확인되어 수술 후 definitive-intent 방사선치료를 시행했다. 초기 추적 영상에서는 국소 재발 소견이 없었으나, 방사선치료 완료 약 9개월 후 진행성 골반 뒷다리 paraparesis, 대변실금, 요저류가 발생했다. MRI에서 L5부터 sacrum까지 이어지는 대형의 조영증강 extradural spinal lesion이 intramedullary invasion 의심 소견과 함께 확인되었고 lumbosacral nerve root의 뚜렷한 비후도 동반되었다. Hemilaminectomy와 척수 생검을 통한 수술적 탐색에서 원발 종양과 조직학적으로 유사한 저분화 sarcoma가 확인되었다. 추적 영상에서 진행성 질환이 확인되어 완화적 방사선치료를 시행했으나 신경학적으로 급격히 악화되어 안락사했다.

## English summary
A 6-year-old, neutered male domestic shorthair cat was referred for a recurrent subcutaneous mass on the right caudodorsolateral hip 4 months after excision of a presumed feline injection-site sarcoma (FISS). Advanced imaging showed a bilobed soft tissue mass closely associated with the sciatic nerve and rectum. Surgical excision was limited by adjacent structures, and histopathology confirmed sarcoma with incomplete margins; definitive-intent radiation therapy followed. Follow-up imaging initially showed no local recurrence, but approximately 9 months after completing radiation therapy the cat developed progressive pelvic limb paraparesis, fecal incontinence, and urinary retention. MRI identified a large contrast-enhancing extradural spinal lesion extending from L5 to the sacrum with suspected intramedullary invasion and marked thickening of the lumbosacral nerve roots. Surgical exploration with hemilaminectomy and spinal cord biopsy revealed a poorly differentiated sarcoma histologically similar to the original tumor. Follow-up imaging confirmed progressive disease; palliative radiation was given, but the cat deteriorated neurologically and was euthanized.

## 임상 시사점
FISS 유사 fibrosarcoma를 sciatic nerve 등 주요 말초신경 인근에서 불완전 절제한 병력이 있는 고양이가 원발 부위 재발 없이 신경학적 증상(파라파레시스, 배변·배뇨 이상)으로 나타나면, perineural spread를 통한 원위부(척수) 침범 가능성을 감별진단에 포함하고 척추 MRI로 extradural·intramedullary 병변을 평가할 필요가 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로, 진단 초기 "advanced imaging"의 구체적 modality(CT 또는 MRI)는 명시되지 않았고 척수 병변 확인 시점의 MRI 소견만 상세히 기술되었다. 단일 증례이므로 perineural spread를 통한 척수 침범의 발생 빈도나 전형적 MRI 소견은 이 증례만으로 일반화할 수 없다.

## 원문 초록
CASE SUMMARY: A 6-year-old, neutered male domestic shorthair cat was referred for evaluation of a recurrent subcutaneous mass on the right caudodorsolateral hip, 4 months after excision of a presumed feline injection site sarcoma (FISS). Advanced imaging revealed a bilobed soft tissue mass closely associated with the sciatic nerve and rectum. Surgical excision was limited by adjacent structures, and histopathology confirmed a sarcoma with incomplete margins. Definitive-intent radiation therapy was administered postoperatively. Follow-up imaging initially demonstrated no evidence of local recurrence. Approximately 9 months after completion of radiation therapy, the cat developed progressive pelvic limb paraparesis, fecal incontinence and urinary retention. MRI identified a large contrast-enhancing extradural spinal lesion extending from L5 to the sacrum, with suspected intramedullary invasion and marked thickening of the lumbosacral nerve roots. Surgical exploration with hemilaminectomy and spinal cord biopsy revealed a poorly differentiated sarcoma histologically similar to the original tumor. Progressive disease was confirmed on follow-up imaging, and palliative radiation therapy was administered. Despite treatment, the cat developed severe neurologic deterioration and was humanely euthanized.

RELEVANCE AND NOVEL INFORMATION: This report describes the first documented case of spinal cord invasion secondary to a FISS-like fibrosarcoma without local recurrence at the primary site. Perineural invasion along the sciatic nerve is proposed as a biologically plausible mechanism of tumor extension. This case highlights an uncommon but severe pattern of disease progression and underscores the importance of considering perineural spread in incompletely excised FISS-like tumors associated with major peripheral nerves.
