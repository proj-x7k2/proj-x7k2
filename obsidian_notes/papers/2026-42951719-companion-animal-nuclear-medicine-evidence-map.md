# Companion animal nuclear medicine: A translational evidence map for dogs and cats.

**Seminars in nuclear medicine (2026)** · 종설(systematic evidence map), 228편 포함 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42951719/) · DOI: 10.1053/j.semnuclmed.2026.10.005

## 국문 요약
PubMed(MEDLINE)와 Web of Science 등을 검색한 PRISMA 기반 systematic evidence map으로, 개·고양이의 nuclear medicine imaging 및 radionuclide therapy 문헌 228편을 정리했다. 176편은 imaging만, 34편은 therapy만, 18편은 둘 다를 다뤘다. 고양이는 thyroid scintigraphy와 radioiodine therapy가, 개는 oncology PET/CT, osteosarcoma, renal·hepatobiliary functional scintigraphy, portosystemic shunt imaging, neurology 영상, thyroid carcinoma, radiosynoviorthesis 등이 주를 이뤘다. 연구 간 이질성 때문에 meta-analysis는 시행하지 않았다.

## English summary
This PRISMA-aligned systematic evidence map included 228 articles on nuclear medicine imaging and radionuclide therapy in dogs and cats (176 imaging-only, 34 therapy-only, 18 both). Feline work centred on thyroid scintigraphy and radioiodine therapy; canine work was broader, with PET/CT most prominent in oncology. Because of heterogeneity, no meta-analysis was performed. The authors conclude that feline thyroid scintigraphy/radioiodine is the most mature application, while canine oncology, molecular imaging and radiotheranostics are the most dynamic future directions.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 갑상선 질환 외에는 개 종양 PET/CT를 포함해 근거가 아직 성장 중인 영역이므로, 핵의학 의뢰를 고려할 때 고양이 hyperthyroidism의 scintigraphy·radioiodine이 가장 확립된 선택지라는 정도로 이해하면 된다. 초록에 효과 크기나 민감도 수치는 없다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
문헌 지도(evidence map)로 개별 연구의 질 평가나 정량 종합은 초록에 나타나지 않으며, 포함 문헌의 대부분이 소규모 연구일 가능성이 있으나 이는 초록에서 확인되지 않는 추정이다. 초록 기반으로만 검토했다.

## 원문 초록
Companion animals occupy an increasingly important clinical and societal space, with dogs and cats often managed as family members and receiving advanced diagnostic and therapeutic care. Many veterinary nuclear medicine procedures are adapted from human practice, while naturally occurring canine and feline diseases provide opportunities for comparative and translational insight. This review mapped and synthesised the literature on nuclear medicine imaging and radionuclide therapy in dogs and cats, with emphasis on clinically mature applications and relevance to human nuclear medicine. A PRISMA-aligned systematic evidence map with focused narrative synthesis were undertaken. MEDLINE via PubMed and Web of Science Core Collection were searched, with supplementary Google Scholar searching, reference-list screening and citation mining. Because of heterogeneity in species, indication, radiopharmaceutical, protocol, comparator and outcome reporting, formal meta-analysis was not performed. A total of 228 articles were included. The literature was predominantly imaging-focused, with 176 articles addressing imaging alone, 34 therapy alone and 18 both imaging and therapy. Feline nuclear medicine was dominated by thyroid disease, particularly thyroid scintigraphy and radioiodine therapy for hyperthyroidism. Canine nuclear medicine was broader, including oncology PET/CT, osteosarcoma imaging and therapy, renal and hepatobiliary functional scintigraphy, portosystemic shunt imaging, neurology/behavioural imaging, thyroid carcinoma and radiosynoviorthesis. PET/CT was most prominent in canine oncology, while conventional scintigraphy remained important where functional information could not be replaced by structural imaging. Companion animal nuclear medicine is clinically useful and translationally relevant. Its most mature model is feline thyroid scintigraphy and radioiodine therapy, while its most dynamic future directions lie in canine oncology, molecular imaging and radiotheranostics. Closer collaboration between veterinary specialists and human nuclear medicine professionals may improve companion animal care while informing broader development of molecular imaging and targeted radionuclide therapy.
