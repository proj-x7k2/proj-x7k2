# Machine-driven vs. manual nickel-titanium instrumentation of canine teeth in dogs: a high resolution micro-CT evaluation of canal geometry and obturation quality.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=30 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42807202/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.joen.2017.07.025
관련 주제: [[topics/head-neck-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
발치된 개 canine teeth 30개를 3군(n=10)으로 나누어 machine-driven nickel-titanium(Ni-Ti) instrumentation 2종과 manual Ni-Ti instrumentation을 비교한 ex vivo 실험 연구다. Pre-/post-treatment micro-CT로 canal geometry, post-to-pre canal volume ratio, internal air volume(obturation 균질성), instrument-canal diameter 불일치를 정량 평가하고, post-treatment CBCT의 진단적 가치도 함께 평가했다. Machine-driven 시스템은 manual instrumentation 대비 instrument-canal diameter 불일치가 유의하게 작았고(p=0.008) internal air volume도 더 적어(p=0.017) 더 균질한 obturation을 보였다. CBCT에서 확인된 dentin-filling interface의 radiolucent zone은 micro-CT로 대조한 결과 실제 void·debris가 아니라 영상 artifact로 밝혀졌다.

## English summary
Thirty extracted canine teeth from dogs were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 10) and instrumented with two machine-driven Ni-Ti systems or manual Ni-Ti hand files, with pre- and post-treatment micro-CT used to quantify canal geometry, volume ratio, air inclusion, and instrument-canal diameter discrepancy. Machine-driven systems showed significantly smaller diameter discrepancies and less internal air volume than manual instrumentation, indicating more homogeneous obturation, despite similar post-to-pre canal volume ratios. Procedural complications occurred in 26.7% of specimens, more often with the less experienced operator. Postoperative CBCT showed radiolucent zones at the dentin-filling interface that micro-CT confirmed were imaging artifacts rather than true voids or debris, cautioning against over-interpreting such CBCT findings.

## 임상 시사점
수의 root canal 치료 후 CBCT에서 dentin-filling interface에 radiolucent zone이 보여도 이것이 반드시 실제 결함을 의미하지는 않으므로, 임상 증상·추가 소견 없이 CBCT 소견만으로 재치료를 결정하지 않도록 주의가 필요하다. 가능하다면 machine-driven Ni-Ti instrumentation이 더 균질한 obturation을 만들 수 있다는 점도 참고할 만하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
발치된 치아 30개(3군, n=10)를 이용한 ex vivo 실험 연구로, 생체 내 치주·혈류·염증 요인은 반영되지 않았다. Procedural complication이 26.7%에서 발생했고 경험이 적은 operator에서 더 흔했다는 점에서 operator 숙련도가 교란 요인으로 작용했을 수 있다. CBCT의 진단적 가치 평가는 micro-CT를 기준으로 한 artifact 확인에 국한되며, 실제 임상 환자의 재발률·치료 결과와는 직접 연관되지 않는다.

## 원문 초록
Root canal treatment in canine teeth of dogs represents a technically demanding procedure due to complex root canal anatomy and large canal dimensions. The present study aimed to compare different endodontic instrumentation techniques and to evaluate their effects on canal geometry, obturation quality, and procedural safety using high-resolution micro-computed tomography (micro-CT). In addition, the diagnostic value of cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) in the assessment of postoperative findings was investigated. Thirty extracted canine teeth were randomly assigned to three groups (n = 10) and instrumented using two different machine-driven nickel-titanium (Ni-Ti) systems or manual Ni-Ti hand files. Pre- and post-treatment micro-CT scans were used for quantitative analysis of canal morphology, post-to-pre canal volume ratio, air inclusion, and discrepancies between canal diameter and instrument size. CBCT imaging was performed for post-treatment evaluation. Mixed-model analysis revealed a significant overall effect of instrumentation group on the discrepancy between canal diameter and instrument size (p = 0.008). Pairwise comparisons demonstrated significantly greater discrepancies for manual instrumentation than for one of the machine-driven systems, whereas the remaining comparisons were not statistically significant after Sidak correction. No significant differences were observed in the post-to-pre canal volume ratio between groups (p = 0.464). A significant group effect was identified for internal air volume (p = 0.017), with manual instrumentation being associated with greater internal air volume, indicating less homogeneous obturation. Procedural complications occurred in 26.7% of specimens and were more frequent for the less experienced operator. CBCT imaging demonstrated radiolucent zones at the dentin-filling interface that were not confirmed as voids or debris by micro-CT and were therefore attributed to imaging artifacts. Machine-driven Ni-Ti systems provided more reproducible canal preparation and were associated with more homogeneous obturation despite achieving a comparable post-to-pre canal volume ratio. Moreover, high-resolution micro-CT demonstrated that radiolucent CBCT findings at the dentin-filling interface predominantly reflected imaging-related artifacts rather than true treatment deficiencies, highlighting the importance of cautious interpretation of postoperative CBCT examinations in veterinary endodontics.
