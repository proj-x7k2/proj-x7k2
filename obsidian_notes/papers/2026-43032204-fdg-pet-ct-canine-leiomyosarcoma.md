# Case Report: 18F-FDG PET/CT for precision assessment of tumor aggressiveness in canine subcutaneous leiomyosarcoma.

**Frontiers in Veterinary Science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43032204/)
관련 주제: [[topics/nuclear-medicine-imaging]], [[topics/oncologic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
9세 intact 수컷 Alaskan Malamute의 우측 elbow 출혈성 궤양성 종괴에서 biopsy는 grade 2 soft tissue sarcoma였으나 임상 경과가 더 공격적이어서 18F-FDG PET/CT를 시행했다. 종괴는 높은 대사 활성(SUVmax 10.1, SUVmean 4.6)을 보였고 원격 전이는 없었다. 수술 후 조직병리는 grade 3 poorly differentiated 종양(incomplete margin)이었고 면역조직화학으로 subcutaneous leiomyosarcoma가 확인되어, 수술 전 biopsy가 grade를 과소평가했고 높은 FDG avidity가 최종 grade와 더 부합했다. 수술 3개월 후 국소 재발과 폐 전이 의심 병변이 나타나 6개월 후 사망했다.

## English summary
A 9-year-old Alaskan Malamute with a rapidly progressing elbow mass had a grade 2 soft tissue sarcoma on biopsy, but 18F-FDG PET/CT showed high uptake (SUVmax 10.1, SUVmean 4.6) without distant metastasis. Post-resection histopathology showed a grade 3 subcutaneous leiomyosarcoma, indicating the biopsy underestimated grade and that the high FDG avidity matched the final grade. Local recurrence and suspected pulmonary metastases appeared 3 months postoperatively, and the dog died 6 months after surgery.

## 임상 시사점
Biopsy grade와 임상 거동이 어긋나는 soft tissue sarcoma에서는 FDG PET/CT의 SUV가 공격성 평가에 참고가 될 수 있으나, 단일 증례이므로 SUV로 grade를 대체하지 말고 biopsy 과소평가 가능성을 염두에 두고 병기 결정(흉부 CT 포함)과 마진 계획을 보수적으로 세우는 것이 합당하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로 SUV cut-off나 민감도·특이도를 도출할 수 없다. PET/CT와 최종 grade의 일치는 한 예의 관찰이며, 보조요법이 시행되지 않아 SUV와 예후의 관계도 일반화할 수 없다. 참고: PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않아 `doi`를 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
A 9-year-old intact male Alaskan Malamute was presented with a hemorrhagic, ulcerative skin lesion on the right elbow. Cytological examination revealed bacterial infection and atypical epithelioid-appearing cells exhibiting anisocytosis and anisokaryosis; however, mesenchymal neoplastic cells were not identified. Despite antimicrobial therapy, the lesion progressed rapidly. Histopathological examination of the biopsy specimen was consistent with a grade 2 soft tissue sarcoma; however, the clinical behavior was more aggressive than anticipated for this grade, prompting further evaluation. 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) demonstrated high metabolic activity of the mass, with a maximum standardized uptake value of 10.1 and mean standardized uptake value of 4.6, with no evidence of distant metastasis. Surgical resection was performed about 2 weeks after PET/CT. Notably, histopathological examination of the surgical specimen revealed a grade 3 poorly differentiated mesenchymal neoplasm with incomplete surgical margins, indicating that the preoperative biopsy had underestimated the final tumor grade and that the high 18F-FDG avidity observed on PET/CT was more consistent with the final histopathologic grade. Immunohistochemical staining confirmed the diagnosis of subcutaneous leiomyosarcoma. Although adjuvant chemotherapy was recommended, only palliative therapy was provided at the owner's request. Local recurrence and pulmonary lesions suspected to be metastatic developed 3 months postoperatively, and the dog expired 6 months after surgery. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first report of 18F-FDG PET/CT findings in canine subcutaneous leiomyosarcoma, underscoring the utility of this imaging modality in evaluating tumor aggressiveness and guiding treatment planning.
