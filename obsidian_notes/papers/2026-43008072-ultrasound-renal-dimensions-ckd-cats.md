# Ultrasound-measured renal length and corticomedullary thickness for the diagnosis and staging of chronic kidney disease in cats.

**Journal of veterinary internal medicine (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=259 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43008072/) · DOI: 없음(원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않아 비워 둠)
관련 주제: [[topics/urogenital-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
건강 고양이 179마리, CKD 38마리, nonazotemic hyperthyroid 42마리의 초음파 renal length, cortical·medullary thickness와 파생 비율을 후향적으로 측정한 연구이다. 건강 고양이에서 나이·체중·성별이 측정값의 생물학적 변이를 설명했고 경미한 좌우 비대칭이 있었다. CKD 고양이는 건강·hyperthyroid 고양이와 구분되었으며, 연령이 비슷한 건강 고양이 대비 kidney가 짧고(-0.49 cm, -13.26%) cortex(dorsal -16.23%)와 medulla(dorsal -26.25%)가 얇았다. Renal length와 medullary thickness의 진단 정확도가 가장 높았지만 어떤 지표도 CKD 중증도를 신뢰성 있게 반영하지 못했고, 관찰자 간 변동이 컸다.

## English summary
In 259 cats (179 healthy, 38 CKD, 42 nonazotemic hyperthyroid), ultrasonographic renal length, cortical and medullary thickness, and ratios were measured retrospectively. Age, body weight and sex explained biological variation in healthy cats. CKD cats had shorter kidneys (-0.49 cm, -13.26%), thinner cortices and thinner medullae than age-comparable healthy cats and were distinguishable from healthy and hyperthyroid cats. Renal length and medullary thickness had the highest diagnostic accuracy, but no dimension reliably reflected severity, and interobserver variability was considerable.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 CKD 평가에서 renal length와 medullary thickness는 진단 보조 지표로 쓸 수 있고 nonazotemic hyperthyroid와도 구분되지만, 병기 판정에는 쓸 수 없다. 나이·체중 효과와 관찰자 간 변동이 크므로 단일 측정값의 cut-off로 판단하지 말고, 같은 환자의 추적 비교나 임상병리와 함께 해석하는 것이 안전하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
후향적 연구로 CKD군(n=38)이 건강군(n=179)보다 훨씬 작다. 관찰자 간 변동은 novice vs expert 비교로 평가되었고 "considerable"하다고 보고되었다. ROC의 구체적 AUC·cut-off 수치는 초록에 없다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Ultrasonography is well established in veterinary practice, and renal dimensions may aid in diagnosing chronic kidney disease (CKD) in cats.

HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: Assess whether renal length, cortical and medullary thickness, and derived ratios identify CKD, reflect CKD severity, and are changed by hyperthyroidism. Evaluate effects of age, body weight, sex, laterality, and observer variability on renal dimensions.

ANIMALS: Two hundred fifty-nine client-owned cats: healthy (n = 179), CKD-affected (n = 38), and nonazotemic hyperthyroid (n = 42).

METHODS: Ultrasonographic renal dimensions were measured retrospectively. Group comparisons were performed using fixed effects and mixed models. Diagnostic accuracy was assessed using receiver operating characteristic analysis. Effects of age, body weight, sex, and laterality were evaluated using multiple linear regression. Intraobserver (novice) and interobserver (novice vs expert) variability were quantified using intraclass correlation coefficients from random effect models.

RESULTS: Age, body weight, and sex accounted for biological variation in healthy cats, which also exhibited minor left-right kidney asymmetry. Cats with CKD were reliably distinguished from healthy and hyperthyroid cats by renal dimensions. Compared with age-comparable healthy cats, CKD-affected cats had shorter kidneys (-0.49 cm, -13.26%, P < .001), thinner cortices (dorsal: -0.10 cm, -16.23%, P < .001; transverse: -0.06 cm, -9.14%, P < .05), and thinner medullae (dorsal: -0.28 cm, -26.25%, P < .001; transverse: -0.24 cm, -19.7%, P < .001). Renal length and medullary thickness showed the highest diagnostic accuracy, but no renal dimension reliably reflected disease severity. Interobserver variability was considerable.

CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Ultrasonographic renal dimensions may support CKD diagnosis in cats, but biological and observer variability must be considered.
