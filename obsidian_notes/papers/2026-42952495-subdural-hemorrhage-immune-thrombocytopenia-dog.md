# Case Report: Presumptive subdural hemorrhage as a rare neurological manifestation of primary immune-mediated thrombocytopenia in a dog.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42952495/)
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
발작과 급성 진행성 intracranial sign을 보인 10세 중성화 암컷 믹스견에서 ecchymoses, petechiae, 양측 retinal hemorrhage와 심한 thrombocytopenia(9 × 10³/μL)가 확인되어 primary immune-mediated thrombocytopenia(ITP)로 진단되었다. Brain MRI에서 양측 cerebral hemisphere를 덮는 minimally expanded crescent 모양의 extra-axial fluid accumulation이 보였고, T1-weighted·T2-weighted hyperintensity와 T2*-weighted GRE의 peripheral susceptibility artifact를 근거로 late subacute subdural hemorrhage로 진단했다. 마취·회복은 순조로웠으나 이후 급성 신경학적 악화와 cardiopulmonary arrest가 와서 사망했으며, 지속 출혈 또는 brain herniation이 의심되었다.

## English summary
A 10-year-old spayed mixed-breed dog with seizures and progressive intracranial signs had severe thrombocytopenia (9 × 10³/μL) and was diagnosed with primary ITP. MRI showed bilateral crescent-shaped extra-axial fluid over both cerebral hemispheres, T1W/T2W hyperintense with peripheral susceptibility artifact on T2*-GRE, consistent with late subacute subdural hemorrhage. After uneventful anesthesia the dog deteriorated acutely and died of cardiopulmonary arrest, suspected from ongoing hemorrhage or herniation.

## 임상 시사점
혈소판 감소가 의심되는 개에서 발작·진행성 신경 징후가 있으면 MRI에 T2*-weighted GRE(또는 SWI)를 반드시 포함해 subdural/extra-axial hemorrhage를 확인하고, 혈소판 수치가 확인되기 전에는 MRI 마취 전 platelet count를 먼저 확보하는 것이 좋다. 이 증례처럼 마취 후 급격히 악화될 수 있어 보호자에게 위험을 미리 설명해야 한다(이 부분은 일반 임상 판단이며 초록이 직접 제시한 권고는 아니다).

## 연구 설계와 한계

## 원문 초록
A 10-year-old female spayed mixed-breed dog was presented with a history of seizures and acute progressive intracranial signs. The neurological examination was consistent with multifocal brain disease. Clinical examination revealed ecchymoses, petechiae, and bilateral retinal hemorrhage. Clinicopathological evaluation identified severe thrombocytopenia [a platelet count of 9 × 103/μL without clumps on manual smear evaluation] with an increased mean platelet volume. Coagulation testing (activated partial thromboplastin time and prothrombin time) and fibrinolysis markers (D-dimers, quantitative fibrinogen), infectious disease screening, and thoracic and abdominal imaging were within normal limits, establishing a diagnosis of primary immune-mediated thrombocytopenia (ITP). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the brain showed minimally expanded, bilateral crescent-shaped, extra-axial fluid accumulations overlying both cerebral hemispheres. Based on T1-weighted and T2-weighted hyperintensities and peripheral susceptibility artifact on T2*-weighted gradient-recalled echo (GRE) imaging, a late subacute subdural intracranial hemorrhage was diagnosed. Despite initial uneventful anesthesia and recovery, the dog experienced acute neurological deterioration and suffered cardiopulmonary arrest, suspected due to ongoing hemorrhage or brain herniation. This report describes a rare neurological manifestation of primary ITP in a dog. While canine ITP generally carries a favorable prognosis, life-threatening secondary intracranial hemorrhage can occur. Primary ITP should be considered as a differential diagnosis for an acute, progressive neurological presentation until a normal platelet count is confirmed.
