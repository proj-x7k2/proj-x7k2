# Dogs and cats undergoing surgery for penetrating thoracic trauma have high survival despite frequent complications, and imaging may underestimate injury severity.

**Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=91 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42826750/) · DOI: 10.2460/javma.26.06.0466
관련 주제: [[topics/trauma-forensic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
5개 referral hospital의 기록을 검토해 수술로 확진된 penetrating thoracic trauma 개 86마리·고양이 5마리(총 91마리)의 임상·방사선 소견과 예후의 연관성을 평가한 다기관 후향적 연구다. 흉부 방사선에서 pneumothorax(73.6%), pulmonary contusion(65.9%), rib fracture(62.6%)가 흔히 확인되었으나, computed tomography를 시행한 경우 방사선보다 더 많은 손상이 확인되는 경우가 많았다. Lung lobectomy는 24.2%에서 시행되었고 비생존군에서 더 흔했다(45.0% vs 18.3%). 생존율은 78.0%(91마리 중 71마리)였고, animal trauma triage score가 비생존군에서 유의하게 높았다(7.6±2.4 vs 5.2±2.5).

## English summary
This retrospective, multicenter study reviewed records from 5 referral hospitals to identify 86 dogs and 5 cats with surgically confirmed penetrating thoracic trauma, evaluating associations between clinical/radiographic findings and outcomes. Radiographic findings included pneumothorax (73.6%), pulmonary contusions (65.9%), and rib fractures (62.6%), while computed tomography often identified more injuries than radiographs. Lung lobectomy was performed in 24.2% of patients and was more common in nonsurvivors (45.0% vs 18.3%). Survival to discharge was 78.0% (71 of 91), and animal trauma triage scores were significantly higher in nonsurvivors (7.6 ± 2.4 vs 5.2 ± 2.5).

## 임상 시사점
Penetrating thoracic trauma 환자에서 흉부 방사선만으로는 실제 손상 범위(특히 폐·흉벽 손상)를 과소평가할 수 있으므로, 임상적으로 중증도가 의심되거나 수술 계획이 필요한 경우 CT로 추가 평가하는 것이 손상 범위 파악과 치료 계획에 더 유리할 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
5개 referral hospital에서 2010년 7월부터 2025년 7월까지 수술로 확진된 penetrating thoracic trauma 개·고양이를 대상으로 한 후향적, 다기관 관찰 연구로, 흉부 방사선·수술 기록·주산기 기록을 검토했다. 고양이 수가 5마리로 매우 적어 종간 비교는 제한적이고, CT는 일부 환자에서만 시행되어 방사선 대비 CT의 추가 진단 가치를 정량적으로 비교하지는 못했다.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVE: To describe clinical and radiographic findings in dogs and cats with surgically confirmed penetrating thoracic trauma and evaluate associations with outcomes.

METHODS: This retrospective, multicenter observational study reviewed records from 5 referral hospitals to identify dogs and cats with surgically confirmed penetrating thoracic trauma between July 1, 2010, and July 31, 2025. Inclusion required thoracic radiographs, surgical reports, and perioperative records.

RESULTS: A total of 86 dogs and 5 cats were identified. Mean animal trauma triage score was higher in nonsurvivors (7.6 ± 2.4; n = 20) than survivors (5.2 ± 2.5; 71). Radiographic findings included pneumothorax (73.6%), pulmonary contusions (65.9%), and rib fractures (62.6%). Computed tomography findings often identified more injuries. Time from presentation to surgery did not differ in survivors versus nonsurvivors. Lung lobectomy was performed in 24.2% of patients and was more common in nonsurvivors (45.0% vs 18.3%). Intra- and postoperative complications occurred in 56% and 51.7% of patients, respectively. Survival to discharge was 78.0% (71 of 91 patients), with a median hospitalization of 3.3 days (range, 1 to 12 days). Among 55 patients with follow up, 11 (20%) had complications related to thoracic trauma.

CONCLUSIONS: Surgical management of penetrating thoracic trauma was associated with a high survival rate despite frequent complications. Animal trauma triage is a useful prognostic indicator. Computed tomography may alter treatment plans.

CLINICAL RELEVANCE: Clinicians should anticipate higher injury severity than anticipated based on routine imaging and examination. Higher animal trauma triage scores were associated with nonsurvival and may provide useful prognostic information. Close monitoring is needed following surgery for penetrating thoracic trauma.
