# Extended Reality for Image-Guided Small Animal Surgery: Current Evidence, Clinical Applications, and Future Directions.

**Animals (2026)** · 종설(narrative review) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42883089/) · DOI: 10.3390/ani16193120
관련 주제: [[topics/imaging-technique-safety]]

## 국문 요약
CT, MRI, ultrasonography, fluoroscopy 기반 image-guided surgery에 extended reality(XR: augmented·virtual·mixed reality)를 접목한 소동물 문헌을 정리한 narrative review다. PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science에서 2026년 5월까지의 문헌을 검색했다. XR 연구는 교육·해부 학습·영상 교육에 집중되어 있고, 실제 임상 수술에 대한 직접 평가는 상대적으로 적었다. 정형, 종양, 신경, 심혈관, 최소침습 시술에서 적용 가능성이 기술되었으나 peer-reviewed 근거는 개에 국한되고 고양이 연구는 확인되지 않았다.

## English summary
This narrative review summarises extended reality (XR) technologies in small animal image-guided surgery, searching PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science up to May 2026. Most studies address education and anatomical training; few assess direct clinical surgical use. Potential applications span orthopedic, oncologic, neurologic, cardiovascular and minimally invasive procedures, but the available evidence is restricted to dogs, with no eligible feline studies.

## 임상 시사점
현재 XR은 임상 도입 단계가 아니며 내일 촬영·수술 프로토콜을 바꿀 근거는 없다. 다만 CT/MRI 3D 재구성을 수술 계획에 활용하는 흐름이 있다는 점은 촬영 시 thin-slice 획득 등 후처리 가능성을 고려하게 한다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
Narrative review로 체계적 선택·비뚤림 평가 방법이 초록에 제시되지 않았다. 저자들은 임상 효과, 비용 효과, 수술 결과에 대한 전향적 연구가 필요하다고 명시했다.

## 원문 초록
Image-guided surgery has become an integral component of modern veterinary surgery using advanced imaging modalities, such as computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, ultrasonography, and fluoroscopy. Recently, extended reality (XR) technologies have emerged as promising tools to enhance image-guided surgical procedures by improving three-dimensional anatomical understanding, surgical navigation, and intraoperative decision-making. Although XR has gained considerable attention in human surgery, its application in veterinary surgery remains at an early stage. This narrative review aimed to provide a comprehensive overview of the current evidence regarding XR technologies in small animal surgery, with particular emphasis on their integration with preoperative and intraoperative imaging modalities. The available literature was reviewed to evaluate current clinical applications, technological developments and future perspectives. A literature search was performed in PubMed, Scopus, and Web of Science for studies published up to May 2026 using combinations of terms related to extended reality and small animal veterinary surgery. Original articles, case reports, pilot studies, technical reports, and reviews addressing clinical, experimental, or simulation-based applications were considered. Although several studies have explored the use of XR technologies for veterinary education, anatomical training, and diagnostic imaging instruction, relatively few investigations have assessed their direct application in clinical surgical practice. Potential applications have been described in orthopedic, oncologic, neurologic, cardiovascular, and minimally invasive procedures. Crucially, the currently available peer-reviewed evidence is restricted to canine patients, as no eligible feline studies were identified. Current evidence suggests that XR is an emerging technology with potential applications in canine surgical planning, image-guided procedures, and surgical training. Further prospective clinical studies are required to establish its clinical effectiveness, cost-effectiveness, and impact on surgical outcomes.
