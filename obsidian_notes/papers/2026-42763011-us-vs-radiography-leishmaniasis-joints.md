# Ultrasonographic detection of joint lesions in canine leishmaniasis caused by Leishmania infantum.

**Veterinary journal (London, England : 1997) (2026)** · 전향적 연구, n=43 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42763011/) · DOI: 10.1016/j.tvjl.2026.106887
관련 주제: [[topics/musculoskeletal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Leishmania infantum PCR 양성인 개 43마리를 대상으로 정형외과 신체검사와 관절 초음파검사(USG)를 시행하고, 그중 20마리는 방사선검사도 함께 시행해 두 영상기법의 관절 병변 검출 성능을 비교했다. Tarsal joint 병변은 USG로 86%(37/43)에서 검출된 반면 방사선검사로는 40%(8/20)에서만 검출되었으며, 신체검사를 기준으로 한 민감도는 USG 89.7% 대 방사선검사 46.7%였다. USG로 관절 병변이 확인된 개의 29.7%(11/37)는 임상적 정형외과 징후가 없어 무증상 관절염 검출에도 USG가 유용함을 시사했다. 저자들은 L. infantum 감염견에서 arthropathy 조기 진단을 위한 1차 검사로 관절 USG를 권장했다.

## English summary
Forty-three Leishmania infantum PCR-positive dogs underwent comparative orthopedic examination and joint ultrasonography (USG); 20 also underwent radiography. Tarsal joint lesions were detected by USG in 86% (37/43) of dogs versus 40% (8/20) by radiography, with USG showing 89.7% sensitivity for detecting joint alterations (physical examination as reference) compared with 46.7% for radiography. Among dogs with USG-detected joint lesions, 29.7% (11/37) had no clinical orthopedic signs, highlighting USG's ability to detect subclinical arthropathy. The authors recommend joint USG as a first-line procedure for early diagnosis of arthropathy in dogs with L. infantum infection.

## 임상 시사점
Leishmaniasis 유행 지역 또는 관련 병력이 있는 개에서 관절 병변이 의심되면(또는 무증상이라도) 방사선검사보다 관절 초음파검사를 1차 검사로 우선 고려할 근거가 된다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
L. infantum PCR 양성견 43마리를 대상으로 신체검사·USG(전체)·방사선검사(일부 20마리)를 비교한 전향적 관찰 연구다. Gold-standard 진단법이 없어 신체검사를 참조 기준으로 삼았다는 한계가 있고, 방사선검사는 43마리 중 20마리에서만 시행되어 두 영상기법의 직접 비교 표본이 제한적이다. 일부 증례에서만 관절액세포검사·조직검사로 병리소견을 확인했다.

## 원문 초록
Canine leishmaniasis (CanL), caused by Leishmania infantum, is a multisystemic disease with substantial musculoskeletal involvement. We aimed to assess the usefulness of USG in diagnosing joint abnormalities in dogs with CanL using a clinical approach. Forty-three L. infantum PCR-positive dogs underwent comparative orthopedic physical examination and joint USG; 20 (20/43) also underwent radiography. Tarsal joints were systematically imaged, whereas other joints were imaged if they showed orthopedic clinical signs. Tarsal lesions were detected by joint USG in 86% (37/43) of the dogs, with osteophytes (83.8%), joint effusion (64.9%), and capsular thickening (59.4%) being the most frequent. Radiography detected tarsal lesions in 40% (8/20) of the dogs. As a definitive gold-standard procedure was not available, physical orthopedic examination served as the reference to assess method sensitivity for detecting joint alterations; USG demonstrated 89.7% sensitivity, whereas radiography demonstrated 46.7%. Overall, 86% (37/43) of the dogs had ultrasonographic joint alterations consistent with degenerative processes associated with chronic inflammation. In selected cases, synovial fluid cytology revealed Leishmania amastigotes, and histopathologic findings confirmed the severe inflammatory nature of the lesions, demonstrating synovial membrane thickening and lymphoplasmacytic infiltrates. Among dogs with joint lesions detected by USG, 29.7% (11/37) had no clinical orthopedic signs, underscoring USG's potential to detect subclinical joint involvement. Using this hitherto inedited approach, we conclude that USG outperformed radiography in detecting arthropathy in CanL, including cases without overt clinical signs of joint alteration. This supports recommending joint USG as a first-line procedure for the early diagnosis of arthropathy in dogs with L. infantum infection.
