# Educational utility of a 3D-printed canine hepatic artery phantom for CT bolus tracking training.

**Alternatives to laboratory animals : ATLA (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=18(참여자) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42765932/) · DOI: 10.1177/02611929261489758
관련 주제: [[topics/imaging-technique-safety]]

## 국문 요약
CT angiography(CTA)에서 bolus tracking은 습득 난이도가 높고 시간 창(temporal window)이 좁아 초심자가 숙달하기 어려운 술기다. 이 연구는 생리적 조영제 동태를 재현하는 peristaltic pump 폐회로에 결합한 3D 프린팅 개 hepatic artery 팬텀을 시뮬레이터 기반 훈련 도구로 활용해 CT bolus tracking 숙련도 향상 효과를 평가했다. 경력 수준별(A: 숙련, B: 중급, C: 초심자)로 층화된 참여자 18명이 표준화된 bolus tracking 프로토콜을 3회 반복 시행했다. B·C군에서는 총 조작 시간이 유의하게 단축된 반면 A군은 유의하지 않은 감소를 보였다. 모든 군에서 region of interest(ROI) 배치 시간이 유의하게 개선되었고, 개념 이해도·술기 자신감·해부학적 인지에 대한 주관적 평가도 유의하게 향상되었다.

## English summary
Bolus tracking for CT angiography (CTA) has a steep learning curve and a narrow temporal window, making it challenging for novice trainees. This study evaluated a 3D-printed canine hepatic artery phantom, integrated into a closed-loop circuit with a peristaltic pump to replicate physiologic contrast kinetics, as a simulator-based training tool. Eighteen participants stratified by experience level (A: experienced, B: intermediate, C: beginner) completed three repeated trials of a standardized bolus tracking protocol. Total operation time significantly decreased in Groups B and C, while Group A showed a non-significant reduction. ROI placement time improved significantly across all groups, along with significant gains in self-reported conceptual understanding, procedural confidence, and anatomical recognition.

## 임상 시사점
CT bolus tracking을 새로 훈련하는 인턴·전공의나 테크니션에게는 3D 프린팅 hepatic artery 팬텀 기반 시뮬레이션 훈련이 실제 환자 조영 CT 촬영 전 ROI 배치·타이밍 판단 능력을 안전하게 반복 연습시키는 실무적 대안이 될 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
경력 수준별로 층화한 참여자 18명을 대상으로 한 실험 연구로, 객관적 시간 지표와 주관적 사전·사후 평가·Likert 설문을 함께 사용했다. 팬텀 훈련 효과가 실제 임상 환자에서의 bolus tracking 성공률이나 CTA 진단 품질 향상으로 이어지는지는 이 연구에서 직접 검증되지 않았고, 대조군(팬텀 훈련 미실시군) 비교도 초록에 제시되지 않았다.

## 원문 초록
Computed tomography angiography (CTA) is critical for evaluating abdominal diseases in veterinary patients. However, mastering bolus tracking remains a challenge for novice trainees, due to its steep learning curve and narrow temporal window. We evaluated the effectiveness of a 3D-printed canine hepatic artery phantom as a simulator-based training tool to improve technical proficiency in CT bolus tracking. The phantom was integrated into a closed-loop circuit with a peristaltic pump to replicate physiologic contrast kinetics. Eighteen participants, who were stratified by level of experience (A, experienced; B, intermediate; C, beginner), completed three repeated trials of a standardised bolus tracking protocol. Training efficacy was assessed by using objective timing metrics, and subjective pre- and post-training evaluations and subjective Likert scale-based questionnaires. Total operation time significantly decreased in Groups B and C, while Group A showed a non-significant reduction. Significant improvements in region of interest (ROI) placement time were observed across all groups. Furthermore, participants reported significant increases in conceptual understanding, procedural confidence and anatomical recognition. Use of the 3D-printed canine hepatic artery phantom is a feasible ethical alternative to the use of animals for such training purposes, effectively standardising technical proficiency in veterinary radiology. The study findings support the integration of high-fidelity phantom use into veterinary radiology curricula, to bridge the gap between theory and clinical practice.
