# Idiopathic facial neuropathy in cats: clinical presentation, magnetic resonance imaging findings, and outcome.

**Journal of Veterinary Internal Medicine (2026)** · 후향적 연구(다기관), n=13 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42873828/) · DOI: 10.1056/NEJMoa072006
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
idiopathic facial neuropathy(IFN)로 presumptive 진단된 고양이 13마리(중앙 연령 30개월)의 다기관 후향적 연구다. 편측 10마리, 양측 3마리(16 sides)였고 palpebral reflex 소실 11/16, 감소 2/16이었으며 8마리에 vestibular sign이 동반되었다. MRI에서 facial nerve thickening 4/16, contrast enhancement 7/16, digastricus muscle caudal belly 감소 9/16이 관찰되었다. CSF는 11마리 중 10마리에서 정상이었다. 추적 가능한 9마리에서 최대 회복까지 중앙 20주가 걸렸고, 3마리는 얼굴·입술·귀 이상이 지속되었으며 vestibular sign은 모두 해소되었다.

## English summary
In 13 cats with presumptive idiopathic facial neuropathy, facial paresis/paralysis was unilateral in 10 and bilateral in 3, and 8 cats had concurrent vestibular signs. MRI showed facial nerve thickening (4/16), contrast enhancement (7/16), or both, and a smaller caudal belly of the digastricus muscle (9/16). CSF was normal in 10/11. Among 9 cats with follow-up, median time to maximal recovery was 20 weeks, with persistent deficits in 3 cats.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 facial paresis에서 MRI 이상이 없거나 경미해도 IFN을 배제할 수 없고, facial nerve enhancement·thickening이나 digastricus muscle 위축이 보이면 IFN을 지지하는 소견으로 참고할 수 있다. 다만 다른 원인 배제용 MRI의 목적이 우선이며, 보호자에게는 회복에 수 개월이 걸릴 수 있고 일부는 잔존 결손이 있음을 안내할 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
13마리 다기관 후향적 서술 연구로, MRI는 임상 정보를 모르는 단일 평가자가 판독했다. 진단은 presumptive이며 조직학적 확진이 없고 추적은 설문 기반으로 13마리 중 9마리에서만 가능했다. 대조군이 없어 MRI 소견의 특이도는 알 수 없다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Idiopathic facial neuropathy (IFN) is commonly described in dogs, but information in cats is lacking.

HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: Describe clinical presentation, magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings and outcome in cats diagnosed with IFN.

ANIMALS: Thirteen cats with presumptive diagnosis of IFN.

METHODS: Multicenter retrospective descriptive study with questionnaire-based follow-up. Magnetic resonance imaging was assessed by a single author blinded to clinical signs.

RESULTS: Median age was 30 months (range, 6-142). Median time of onset to presentation was 6 days (range, 1-150). Facial paresis or paralysis was unilateral in 10 cases and bilateral in 3 (16 sides affected). Clinical signs included absent (11/16) or decreased (2/16) palpebral reflex; lip ptosis (5/16); absent or decreased ear movements (3/6 reported). Eight cats had vestibular signs, including head tilt (6/13), vestibular ataxia (6/13), head swaying (2/13), and horizontal nystagmus (3/13). Findings on MRI included thickened (4/16), or contrast-enhancing (7/16) facial nerve or both and smaller caudal belly of the digastricus muscle (9/16). Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) was normal in 10 of 11 cats. Treatments included eye lubrication (11/13) and anti-inflammatory doses of prednisolone (3/13). Follow-up was available for 9 of 13 cats (median, 13 months; range, 2-91). Median time to maximal recovery was 20 weeks (range, 2-24). Recovery of spontaneous blinking was complete in 7 out of 12 eyes, partial in 2 out of 12 and absent in 3 out of 12. Three cats had persistent abnormalities of the lips, face, ears or some combination of these. All vestibular signs resolved but one cat was euthanized because of relapse.

CONCLUSION AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Idiopathic facial neuropathy appears to affect mostly young cats, often unilaterally and frequently with vestibular signs. Spontaneous improvement is likely, but persistent neurologic deficits are possible.
