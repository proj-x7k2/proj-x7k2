# Evaluation of intrarenal hypoperfusion and renal congestion assessed by intrarenal Doppler ultrasonography in dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease.

**Journal of veterinary internal medicine (2026)** · 전향적 연구, n=142 (대조 62 + MMVD 80) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43021594/) · DOI: 없음(원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않아 비워 둠)
관련 주제: [[topics/cardiac-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
심질환이 없는 개 62마리와 myxomatous mitral valve disease(MMVD) 개 80마리를 대상으로, interlobar renal artery의 velocity time integral(VTI)을 intrarenal Doppler로 측정해 renal perfusion 지표로 평가한 전향적 코호트 연구이다. VTI는 심박수와 음의 상관이 있어 보정한 VTIc를 사용했고, MMVD에서 VTIc는 ACVIM stage가 높을수록 감소했다. VTIc <8.3인 개는 생존 기간이 유의하게 짧았고(P<.001), 다변량 Cox 분석에서는 venous impedance index(VII)가 유의하게 유지되었다(HR 1.24/0.1 증가; 95% CI 1.07-1.42). VII(<0.37 vs ≥0.37)와 VTIc(≥8.3 vs <8.3)를 결합한 탐색적 분류는 생존이 다른 4개 subset을 구분했다.

## English summary
In this prospective cohort of 62 dogs without heart disease and 80 dogs with MMVD, interlobar renal artery VTI was measured by intrarenal Doppler and corrected for heart rate (VTIc). VTIc decreased with higher ACVIM stage, and VTIc <8.3 was associated with shorter survival (P<.001), while VII remained significant in multivariable Cox analysis (HR 1.24 per 0.1 increase; 95% CI 1.07-1.42). An exploratory combination of VII and VTIc identified four subsets with different survival.

## 임상 시사점
MMVD 개의 복부 초음파에서 신장 Doppler로 VII에 더해 interlobar artery VTIc를 함께 재면 renal congestion뿐 아니라 hypoperfusion 정보도 얻을 수 있어, cardiorenal 관점의 위험 층화에 쓸 수 있다. 다만 결합 분류는 탐색적이므로 아직 치료 결정의 근거로 쓰기는 이르다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
전향적 코호트이나 단일 연구이고, 1차 평가변수(cardiac·renal death 복합)의 사건 수와 중앙 생존기간이 초록에 충분히 제시되지 않았다(중앙값 not reached). VTIc cut-off 8.3과 4개 subset 분류는 탐색적이며 외부 검증이 없다. VTI는 수동 tracing 방식이라 관찰자 간 재현성이 초록에 없다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: While the predictive value of venous impedance index (VII) for evaluating renal congestion is reported in dogs with myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD), an ultrasonographic index of renal perfusion has not been reported.

HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: Low renal perfusion assessed by interlobar renal artery velocity time integral (VTI) is associated with a worse prognosis in dogs with MMVD. Combining VTI and VII provides additional prognostic information.

ANIMALS: Sixty-two client-owned dogs without heart disease evaluated for preanesthetic or general health assessment and 80 client-owned dogs with MMVD.

METHODS: Prospective cohort study. The VTI was measured by manually tracing the spectral velocity envelope of interlobar arteries. The primary endpoint was defined as the composite of cardiac-related death and renal-related death.

RESULTS: The VTI was corrected for the effect of heart rate (VTIc) because VTI and heart rate were negatively correlated in dogs without heart disease. In dogs with MMVD, the VTIc decreased with higher ACVIM stage. Dogs with lower VTIc (< 8.3) had significantly shorter survival times (median, not reached; 95% CI, 98 days-not reached; P < .001) than those with normal VTIc (median, not reached; 95% CI, not reached). In multivariable Cox analysis, VII remained significantly associated with outcome (HR, 1.24 per 0.1-unit increase; 95% CI, 1.07-1.42). Exploratory classification combining VII (<0.37 or ≥0.37) and VTIc (≥8.3 or < 8.3) identified 4 subsets with different survival outcomes.

CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: The VTIc might be associated with outcome in dogs with MMVD and combined assessment might provide additional exploratory prognostic information.
