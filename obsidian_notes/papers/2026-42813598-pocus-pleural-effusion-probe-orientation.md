# Veterinary point-of-care ultrasound probe orientation and location for detection of pleural effusion in canine cadavers: a pilot study.

**The Journal of small animal practice (2026)** · 실험 연구, n=12 cadavers(12 operators) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42813598/) · DOI: 10.1111/jsap.70196
관련 주제: [[topics/imaging-technique-safety]], [[topics/thoracic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
양압 환기 중인, scant~large volume pleural effusion을 가진 intubated canine cadaver 12구를 sternal recumbency에서 12명의 operator(응급·중환자 전문의 2명, 수의대생 2명, intern 8명)가 두 가지 무작위 프로토콜로 양측 흉부 point-of-care ultrasound를 시행한 pilot study다. Modified TFAST for pleural fluid 기법과 modified Calgary PLUS for pleural fluid 기법을 비교한 결과, 전체 pleural effusion 검출률은 Calgary PLUS 변형 기법이 TFAST 변형 기법보다 유의하게 높았다(89.1% vs 60.9%, P<.001). Scant-to-mild effusion에서는 Calgary PLUS 변형 기법이 유의하게 우수했으나(84.4% vs 37.5%, P<.001) moderate-to-large effusion에서는 두 기법 간 차이가 없었다(93.8% vs 84.4%, P=.25).

## English summary
In this pilot study, 12 intubated canine cadavers with scant to large volume pleural effusion, under positive pressure ventilation in sternal recumbency, were scanned by 12 operators (2 emergency/critical care specialists, 2 veterinary students, 8 interns) using two randomized bilateral thoracic point-of-care ultrasound protocols. Overall detection of pleural effusion was significantly higher with the modified Calgary PLUS for pleural fluid technique than the modified TFAST for pleural fluid technique (89.1% vs 60.9%, P < .001). Detection of scant-to-mild effusion was significantly better with Calgary PLUS (84.4% vs 37.5%, P < .001), but there was no significant difference for moderate-to-large effusions (93.8% vs 84.4%, P = .25).

## 임상 시사점
흉부 POCUS로 pleural effusion을 선별할 때, 특히 소량~경도 삼출(scant-to-mild effusion)이 의심되는 환자에서는 probe를 costochondral junction보다 더 ventral한 부위에서 rib에 평행하게(parallel) 대는 modified Calgary PLUS 방식이 전통적 TFAST 방식보다 검출률이 높을 수 있다. 중등도~대량 삼출에서는 두 기법 간 차이가 크지 않으므로, 소량 삼출을 놓치기 쉬운 상황(외상 초기 평가 등)에서 이 결과를 우선 적용해볼 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
양압 환기 중인 intubated canine cadaver 12구(scant~large volume pleural effusion 보유)를 대상으로, 숙련도가 다른 12명의 operator가 눈가림 없이 두 가지 무작위 스캔 프로토콜(modified TFAST, modified Calgary PLUS)을 순서를 바꿔 시행한 실험 연구다. 생체가 아닌 사체 모델이고 양압 환기라는 특수한 조건이어서 자연호흡 중인 임상 환자에게 그대로 일반화하기는 어렵다. Pilot study로 명시된 만큼 표본(12구)이 작고, 저자들도 이를 한계로 인정했다.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVES: To evaluate whether point-of-care ultrasound probe location (at or below the costochondral junction) and probe orientation (perpendicular or parallel to the ribs) affect operator ability to detect pleural effusion in a canine cadaver model.

MATERIALS AND METHODS: Twelve intubated canine cadavers with scant to large volume pleural effusion were scanned in sternal recumbency while receiving positive pressure ventilation. Twelve operators (2 emergency and critical care specialists, 2 veterinary students and 8 interns) performed blinded bilateral thoracic point-of-care ultrasound using two randomised scanning protocols: a modified TFAST for pleural fluid technique and a modified Calgary PLUS for pleural fluid technique. Hemithoraces were categorised as scant-to-mild or moderate-to-large effusion for statistical analysis. Paired detection rates were compared using McNemar's test.

RESULTS: Overall detection of pleural effusion was significantly higher using the modified Calgary PLUS for pleural fluid technique compared with the modified TFAST for pleural fluid technique (89.1% vs. 60.9%; P < .001). There was a statistically significant increase in the detection of scant-to-mild effusion with the modified Calgary PLUS for pleural fluid technique (84.4% vs. 37.5%; P < .001). No significant difference was observed for moderate-to-large effusions (93.8% vs. 84.4%; P = .25).

CLINICAL SIGNIFICANCE: The site assessed and probe orientation used with different thoracic point-of-care ultrasound protocols can influence the operator's ability to detect pleural effusion in positive pressure ventilation canine cadavers placed in sternal recumbency. Incorporating a probe orientation that is parallel to the ribs in the more ventral regions of the thorax during point-of-care ultrasound may improve the operator's detection of pleural effusions, particularly when smaller volumes of pleural fluid are present.
