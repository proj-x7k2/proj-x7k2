# Clinical Relevance of Routine Follow-Up Computed Tomography in Dogs with Presumed Discospondylitis: A Retrospective Study.

**Veterinary sciences (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=32 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42944152/)
관련 주제: [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Discospondylitis가 의심된 개 32마리의 초기·추적 CT를 board-certified 영상의학 전문의 2인이 독립적으로 판독하고, 사전 정의한 CT 이상 소견의 변화를 합쳐 CT Evolution Score로 만들어 임상 결과와의 연관을 보았다. 개별 CT 소견의 interobserver agreement는 fair~substantial, CT Evolution Score의 신뢰도는 moderate(ICC=0.72)였다. 임상 결과가 좋은 개는 paravertebral soft tissue abnormality(p<0.001)와 presumptive spinal epidural empyema(p=0.002)의 변화가 더 호전적이었던 반면 osseous change는 차이가 없었다. CT Evolution Score는 좋은 결과군에서 높았고(p=0.010), 나쁜 결과의 odds 감소와 연관되었으며(OR 0.40; 95% CI 0.17-0.91; p=0.029), 판별력은 AUC 0.87이었다.

## English summary
Initial and follow-up CT of 32 dogs with suspected discospondylitis were reviewed independently by two board-certified radiologists, and changes in predefined abnormalities were combined into a CT Evolution Score (ICC 0.72). Favourable changes in paravertebral soft tissue (p<0.001) and presumptive epidural empyema (p=0.002), but not osseous changes, were associated with good outcome. A higher score was associated with lower odds of poor outcome (OR 0.40, 95% CI 0.17-0.91) and showed AUC 0.87. Follow-up CT may add information, particularly when clinical improvement is incomplete or unfavourable.

## 임상 시사점
임상적으로 잘 호전되는 discospondylitis에서는 추적 CT를 일률적으로 찍을 필요가 크지 않고, 호전이 불완전하거나 악화될 때 paravertebral soft tissue와 epidural empyema 변화를 중심으로 보는 것이 유용해 보인다. 뼈 변화(osseous change)는 결과와 무관했으므로 추적 시 lysis/sclerosis가 남아 있다는 이유만으로 치료 실패로 해석하지 않는 편이 안전하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 설계의 후향적 연구로 n=32이고 "presumed" discospondylitis라 미생물학적 확진 비율은 초록에 없다. 불일치 소견을 합의로 해결했고 일부 개별 소견의 agreement가 fair 수준이며, CT Evolution Score의 AUC는 같은 자료에서 산출한 것으로 외부 검증이 없다. 추적 CT 시점이 개체마다 달랐는지, 임상 결과 정의 등은 초록에 없다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않는 값이어서 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
Computed tomography (CT) findings of discospondylitis in dogs are well described, but limited information is available regarding the clinical value of follow-up CT in veterinary medicine. This retrospective study evaluated CT changes and their associations with clinical outcome in 32 dogs with suspected discospondylitis. Initial and follow-up CT examinations were independently reviewed by two board-certified veterinary radiologists. Disagreements were resolved by agreement between reviewers, and changes in predefined CT abnormalities were combined into a CT Evolution Score. Interobserver agreement for individual CT features ranged from fair to substantial, while the CT Evolution Score showed moderate reliability (ICC = 0.72). Dogs with a good clinical outcome showed more favorable changes in paravertebral soft tissue abnormalities (p < 0.001) and presumptive spinal epidural empyema (p = 0.002), whereas osseous changes did not differ significantly. The CT Evolution Score was higher in dogs with a good outcome (p = 0.010), associated with lower odds of a poor outcome (OR, 0.40; 95% CI, 0.17-0.91; p = 0.029), and showed good discriminatory ability (AUC = 0.87). Follow-up CT may provide complementary information, particularly in dogs with incomplete or unfavorable clinical improvement.
