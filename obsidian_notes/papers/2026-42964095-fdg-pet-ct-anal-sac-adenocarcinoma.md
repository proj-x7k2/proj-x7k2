# Case Report: 18F-FDG PET/CT findings and SUVmax characterization in a dog with apocrine gland anal sac adenocarcinoma.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42964095/)
관련 주제: [[topics/nuclear-medicine-imaging]], [[topics/oncologic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
우측 anal sac mass와 림프절 전이가 의심된 10세 중성화 수컷 Maltese에서 수술 전 18F-FDG PET/CT를 시행했다. 원발 종괴(SUVmax 7.3), internal iliac lymph node(SUVmax 6.35), lumbar aortic lymph node(SUVmax 4.3)에서 uptake가 증가했고 원격 전이는 없어 Stage IIIa로 분류했다. 복강 내 lymphadenectomy와 우측 anal sacculectomy 후 조직병리에서 원발 종괴와 절제한 림프절의 전이성 adenocarcinoma가 확인되었다. 저자들은 AGASACA에서 PET/CT 소견과 SUVmax를 처음 보고했다고 밝혔다.

## English summary
A 10-year-old castrated Maltese with a right anal sac mass underwent preoperative 18F-FDG PET/CT, showing increased uptake in the primary mass (SUVmax 7.3), internal iliac lymph node (6.35) and lumbar aortic lymph node (4.3) without distant metastasis (Stage IIIa). After lymphadenectomy and sacculectomy, histopathology confirmed AGASACA with metastatic adenocarcinoma in the resected nodes. The authors suggest PET/CT may provide complementary metabolic information for preoperative nodal staging.

## 임상 시사점
AGASACA에서 CT로 크기만 보던 sublumbar·internal iliac 림프절 평가에 FDG uptake 정보를 더할 수 있다는 가능성을 보여주는 증례다. 단일 증례이므로 SUVmax로 전이 여부를 판정하는 기준으로 쓸 수는 없다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로 비전이 림프절과의 SUVmax 비교나 민감도·특이도 평가가 없다. 조직병리 확인은 절제한 림프절에 대해서만 가능하며, 영상에서 uptake가 없었던 림프절의 상태는 초록에 나오지 않는다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 논문과 맞지 않는 값이어서 비워 두었다.

## 원문 초록
Apocrine gland anal sac adenocarcinoma (AGASACA) is a malignant epithelial tumor in dogs that commonly metastasizes to regional lymph nodes. Although 18F-fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) positron emission tomography/computed tomography (PET/CT) has been mentioned as a staging modality for this tumor, detailed imaging findings and maximum standardized uptake values (SUVmax) have not previously been reported. A 10-year-old castrated male Maltese was referred for a right anal sac mass and suspected lymph node metastasis. Preoperative 18F-FDG PET/CT revealed increased uptake in the primary mass (SUVmax 7.3), the internal iliac lymph node (SUVmax 6.35), and the lumbar aortic lymph node (SUVmax 4.3), with no evidence of distant metastasis. Therefore, the dog was therefore classified as Stage IIIa. Based on these findings, intra-abdominal lymphadenectomy and right anal sacculectomy were performed. Histopathology confirmed AGASACA in the primary mass and morphologically similar metastatic adenocarcinoma in the resected lymph nodes. To our knowledge, this is the first report of 18F-FDG PET/CT findings and SUVmax values in a dog with AGASACA and histologically confirmed lymph node metastases. These findings suggest that PET/CT may provide complementary metabolic information for preoperative nodal staging.
