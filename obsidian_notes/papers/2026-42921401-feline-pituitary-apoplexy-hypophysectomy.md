# Case Report: Surgical treatment of feline pituitary apoplexy in 3 cases.

**Frontiers in veterinary science (2026)** · 증례군, n=3 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42921401/) · DOI: 10.1016/B0-72-168903-5/50020-3
관련 주제: [[topics/endocrine-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Transsphenoidal hypophysectomy 후 조직병리로 출혈성 병변을 동반한 pituitary mass가 확인된 고양이 3마리의 후향적 증례 검토다. 2마리는 급성 신경학적 증상, 1마리는 subacute endocrinological dysfunction으로 내원했다. 3마리 모두 영상 소견이 pituitary apoplexy에 합당했으나 hemorrhage-sensitive sequence를 촬영한 것은 case 3뿐이었고, 이 증례에서만 intralesional hemorrhage가 명확히 확인되었다. 모두 수술 후 퇴원했고 생존기간은 최소 716일(재발로 재수술 후 추적 소실), 최소 1,098일(생존 중), 612일(MRI상 365일째 재발 확인, radiation therapy 중 사망)이었다.

## English summary
Three cats with histopathologically confirmed haemorrhagic pituitary masses underwent transsphenoidal hypophysectomy and were reviewed retrospectively. Two presented with acute neurologic signs and one with subacute endocrinological dysfunction. Imaging was compatible with pituitary apoplexy in all three, but only case 3 was imaged with a haemorrhage-sensitive sequence, which provided unequivocal evidence of intralesional haemorrhage. All cats recovered and were discharged; survival was at least 716 days (recurrence, second surgery), at least 1,098 days (alive), and 612 days (MRI recurrence at 365 days, died during radiation therapy).

## 임상 시사점
큰 pituitary mass를 가진 고양이가 급성 신경학적 증상을 보이면 apoplexy를 감별에 두고, MRI protocol에 hemorrhage-sensitive sequence(예: T2*-weighted/GRE, SWI)를 포함시키면 intralesional hemorrhage를 확인할 수 있다. 수술 후에는 잔존 조직에서의 재발이 있을 수 있어 MRI 추적이 필요하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
수술을 받고 조직병리가 확인된 3마리를 의무기록·영상기록으로 후향 검토한 증례군으로, 선택 편향이 불가피하고 수술적 치료의 효과를 비수술 치료와 비교할 수 없다. 영상 protocol이 증례마다 달랐으며 초록에 구체적 sequence명과 영상 소견 상세는 제시되지 않았다. PubMed 원자료의 DOI가 저널과 맞지 않는 값(도서 챕터 형식)이어서 원자료 그대로 기재했다 — 확인 필요.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Surgical treatment of feline pituitary apoplexy has not been previously described.

OBJECTIVES: To describe the clinical course and follow-up of 3 cases of feline pituitary apoplexy.

METHODS: Cases were selected based on histopathological confirmation of pituitary masses with haemorrhagic lesions, following transsphenoidal hypophysectomy. Details of physical examination, diagnostic test results, treatment and follow-up course were retrospectively acquired by review of medical and imaging records.

RESULTS: Two cats were presented with acute onset of neurologic signs (case 1 and 2), and one cat showed subacute endocrinological dysfunction (case 3). All cases underwent successful transsphenoidal hypophysectomy, followed by intensive care hospitalization and successful discharge. Pituitary apoplexy was identified by histopathological examination of the excised pituitary glands in cases 1, 2, and 3. Imaging findings compatible with pituitary apoplexy were present in all 3 cases, although only case 3 was imaged with a hemorrhage-sensitive sequence, providing unequivocal evidence of intralesional hemorrhage. Case 1 had a survival time at least 716 days, underwent a second transsphenoidal surgery because of tumor recurrence and was subsequently lost to follow-up. Case 2 had a survival time of at least 1,098 days and is still alive at the time of writing this manuscript. In case 3, tumor recurrence was identified radiologically (MRI) at 365 days, and it died during a course of radiation therapy 612 days after transsphenoidal hypophysectomy.

CONCLUSION AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Pituitary apoplexy is a rare condition that also affects cats with large pituitary adenomas. Overall, the cats experienced acceptable clinical recovery and postoperative survival, supporting the feasibility of transsphenoidal hypophysectomy as a therapeutic option for cats with pituitary tumors affected by apoplexy. Notably, a favorable outcome was observed even in the presence of remnant pituitary tissue, as illustrated by the cat that later developed pituitary tumor recurrence.
