# Evaluation of Intracranial Lesions Using Deep Learning-Based Reconstruction in Canine Brain MRI: Comparison With Conventional Reconstruction on T2-Weighted and FLAIR Sequences.

**Veterinary radiology & ultrasound : the official journal of the American College of Veterinary Radiology and the International Veterinary Radiology Association (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=25 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42813384/) · DOI: 10.1111/vru.70260
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]], [[topics/ai-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
두개내 질환으로 진단된 개 25예의 brain MRI 검사를 대상으로, deep learning-based reconstruction(DLR)과 conventional reconstruction을 T2-weighted·FLAIR 시퀀스에서 비교한 후향적 연구다. DLR 영상은 두 시퀀스 모두에서 conventional 영상보다 signal-to-noise ratio(SNR)·contrast-to-noise ratio(CNR)·lesion edge sharpness가 유의하게 높았고(p<0.05), 블라인드 판독한 두 명의 radiologist가 평가한 영상 품질·lesion conspicuity 점수도 유의하게 높았다. Truncation artifact는 유의하게 감소했으나 motion·pulsation artifact는 개선되지 않았고, pseudolesion이나 lesion masking 사례는 관찰되지 않았다.

## English summary
This retrospective study compared deep learning-based reconstruction (DLR) with conventional reconstruction on T2-weighted and FLAIR sequences in 25 canine brain MRI examinations diagnosed with intracranial disease. DLR images showed significantly higher SNR, CNR, and lesion edge sharpness than conventional images in both sequences (p < 0.05), along with significantly higher radiologist-rated image quality and lesion conspicuity scores. Truncation artifact was significantly reduced, but motion and pulsation artifacts were not improved, and no instances of pseudolesions or lesion masking were observed.

## 임상 시사점
DLR을 사용할 수 있는 1.5T MRI 환경이라면 두개내 병변이 의심되는 개 환자의 T2-weighted·FLAIR 시퀀스에 DLR을 적용해 병변 가시성과 영상 품질을 향상시킬 수 있지만, motion·pulsation artifact가 있는 환자에서는 DLR만으로 이를 해결하지 못하므로 여전히 진정·환자 고정 등 기존 artifact 관리가 필요하다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
두개내 질환으로 진단된 개 25예의 brain MRI를 대상으로 DLR과 conventional reconstruction을 동일 raw data에서 비교한 후향적 연구로, 두 명의 블라인드 radiologist가 정성 평가를 수행했다. 대상이 두개내 질환이 있는 환자로 한정되어 정상 뇌나 다른 질환군에서의 성능은 알 수 없고, 진단 성능(민감도·특이도) 자체에 대한 평가는 포함하지 않아 추후 연구가 필요하다고 저자들도 명시했다.

## 원문 초록
Obtaining high-resolution brain MRI in small animals is challenging due to the inherent trade-off between image quality and acquisition time. Deep learning-based reconstruction (DLR) has emerged as a solution to improve image quality without prolonging scan time; however, its clinical utility and potential risks, such as pseudolesions, remain insufficiently evaluated in veterinary patients. This study aimed to evaluate the impact of DLR on lesion assessment and image quality in canine brain MRI and to investigate potential limitations associated with its application. This retrospective study compared DLR with conventional reconstruction in 25 canine brain MRI examinations diagnosed with intracranial diseases. Transverse T2-weighted and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery sequences were reconstructed using both conventional and DLR methods. Quantitative analysis included signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), contrast-to-noise ratio (CNR), and lesion edge sharpness. Two blinded radiologists assessed image quality, lesion conspicuity, artifacts, pseudolesions, and lesion masking. DLR images exhibited significantly higher SNR, CNR, and lesion edge sharpness than conventional images in both sequences (p < 0.05). DLR images also yielded significantly higher qualitative scores for image quality and lesion conspicuity (p < 0.05). Truncation artifact was significantly reduced, whereas motion and pulsation artifacts showed no improvement. No instances of pseudolesions or lesion masking were noted. DLR significantly improves image quality and lesion conspicuity in canine brain MRI and reduces truncation artifacts; however, it does not mitigate motion or pulsation interference. Collectively, these findings suggest that DLR has potential value for improving image quality and lesion visibility in canine brain MRI, whereas further studies are needed to determine its impact on diagnostic performance.
