# EXPRESS: BENEFITS OF CONTRAST-ENHANCED COMPUTED TOMOGRAPHY OVER PLAIN COMPUTED TOMOGRAPHY FOR HEAD TRAUMA IN 41 CATS.

**Journal of feline medicine and surgery (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=41 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42934851/) · DOI: 10.1177/1098612X261502912
관련 주제: [[topics/trauma-forensic-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Pre-/post-contrast head CT를 시행한 외상 고양이 41마리의 영상을 경험 수준이 다른 영상의학 판독자 3명이 독립적으로 판독해 extracranial soft tissue injury, bone fracture, intracranial abnormality를 평가하고 Cohen's kappa로 판독자 내·간 일치도를 분석했다. Extracranial soft tissue와 fracture는 substantial~almost perfect 일치도를 보였고, 가장 흔한 골절 부위는 TMJ, hard palate, mandible, pterygoid bone이었다. Hemorrhage·cerebral edema 같은 intracranial lesion은 조영 여부와 판독자 경험에 관계없이 일치도가 낮고 가변적이었으며, 세 범주 모두에서 plain CT와 contrast CT 사이에 유의한 차이는 없었다.

## English summary
Three radiologists of differing experience independently reviewed pre- and post-contrast head CT of 41 trauma cats. Agreement was substantial to almost perfect for extracranial soft tissue injury and fractures (most often TMJ, hard palate, mandible, pterygoid bones), but low and variable for intracranial lesions such as haemorrhage and oedema regardless of contrast or experience. No significant difference was found between plain and contrast CT in any category.

## 임상 시사점
고양이 head trauma의 골절·extracranial soft tissue 평가에는 조영제가 추가 정보를 주지 않았으므로, 이 목적이라면 plain CT로 충분할 수 있다. 반대로 CT만으로 intracranial 출혈·부종을 확정하기는 신뢰도가 낮으므로, 신경학적 증상이 있으면 MRI나 임상 경과 추적을 병행해야 한다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 연구의 후향적 판독자 연구로 n=41이며 판독자 3명이다. 초록에는 임상 결과나 MRI 등 reference standard가 없어 intracranial lesion의 진단 정확도는 평가할 수 없고, 일치도만 측정했다. 조영제가 유용한 특정 소견(예: 감염, 종양)은 이 외상 코호트에서 다루지 않았다.

## 원문 초록
OBJECTIVES: The aim of this study is to evaluate whether contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT) provides clinically useful information beyond Plain computed tomography (PCT) in cats with head trauma, and whether this benefit is influenced by the radiologist's level of experience.

METHODS: Forty-one cats that underwent head CT with both pre- and post-contrast sequences were included. Images were independently reviewed by three radiologists with different experience levels, assessing extracranial soft tissue injuries, bone fractures, and intracranial abnormalities. Intra- and interobserver agreements were analyzed using Cohen's kappa statistics.

RESULTS: Substantial to almost perfect agreement was obtained for extracranial soft tissue injuries and bone fractures. The most commonly fractured sites were the temporomandibular joints, hard palate, mandibular bones, and pterygoid bones. For intracranial lesions such as hemorrhage and cerebral edema, intra- and interobserver agreements were low and variable, regardless of contrast use or reviewer experience. There was no significant difference found between PCT and CECT for any of the three categories.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: These results suggest that contrast medium injection is not useful for assessment of bone fractures and extracranial soft tissue and that computed tomography of the head is poorly reliable for intracranial lesions in cats presented for trauma.
