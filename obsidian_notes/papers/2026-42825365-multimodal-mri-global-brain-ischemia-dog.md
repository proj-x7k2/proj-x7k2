# Multimodal MRI findings in a dog with suspected global brain ischemia and peri-anesthetic blindness.

**Journal of veterinary science (2026)** · 증례보고, n=1 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42825365/) · DOI: 10.4142/jvs.26132
관련 주제: [[topics/neuro-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
전신마취 후 입원 치료를 받고 퇴원 시점에 급성 양측 실명이 확인된 10세 중성화하지 않은 수컷 Welsh Corgi 1예의 증례보고다. Conventional MRI(T2-weighted, FLAIR, diffusion-weighted imaging)에서는 diffuse cerebral swelling과 sulcal effacement만 보였고, global brain ischemia(GBI)의 전형적 소견인 피질 T2W/FLAIR hyperintensity나 명확한 diffusion restriction은 확인되지 않아 진단이 애매했다. Advanced MRI를 추가한 결과, arterial spin labeling(ASL)에서 cortex·thalamus의 뚜렷한 cerebral blood flow(CBF) 저하, dynamic susceptibility contrast(DSC)에서 relative CBF 감소·relative CBV 증가·관류 지연, time-of-flight MR angiography에서 두개내 동맥 조영 저하, diffusion tensor imaging(DTI)에서 피질의 fractional anisotropy·diffusivity 이상이 모두 확인되어 global hypoperfusion과 미세구조 손상이 뒷받침되었다. Mannitol 등 보존적 치료에도 실명은 지속되었고 신경학적 예후는 불량했다.

## English summary
This case report describes a 10-year-old intact male Welsh Corgi with acute bilateral blindness noted at discharge after overnight hospitalization following general anesthesia, in which conventional MRI (T2W, FLAIR, DWI) was equivocal, showing only diffuse cerebral swelling and sulcal effacement without the cortical hyperintensity or clear diffusion restriction typically described in global brain ischemia (GBI). Advanced MRI supported suspected GBI: arterial spin labeling showed markedly reduced cortical and thalamic CBF, dynamic susceptibility contrast showed reduced relative CBF with increased relative CBV and prolonged transit, time-of-flight MR angiography showed poor intracranial arterial conspicuity, and diffusion tensor imaging demonstrated cortical microstructural alterations. Despite mannitol and supportive care, blindness persisted and the dog had a poor neurologic outcome.

## 임상 시사점
Peri-anesthetic 급성 신경학적 이상(특히 실명)을 보인 환자에서 conventional T2W/FLAIR/DWI MRI가 뚜렷하지 않다고 global brain ischemia를 배제할 수 없으며, 장비·시간이 허락한다면 ASL·DSC perfusion, TOF MRA, DTI 같은 advanced MRI 기법을 추가해 global hypoperfusion과 미세구조 손상을 확인하는 것이 진단에 도움이 될 수 있다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
단일 증례보고로, GBI의 전형적 영상 소견(피질 T2W/FLAIR hyperintensity, diffusion restriction)이 없는 비정형 사례를 다룬다. 마취 관련 저혈압·저산소 등 구체적 원인의 인과관계는 증례 특성상 확정할 수 없고, 제시된 advanced MRI 소견의 특이도·재현성은 추가 증례 축적을 통한 검증이 필요하다.

## 원문 초록
IMPORTANCE: Global brain ischemia (GBI) in dogs is diagnosed based on acute neurologic signs, temporal association with a hypoxic-ischemic or anesthetic event, and supportive magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) findings. Cortical T2-weighted (T2W) and fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) hyperintensity and diffusion restriction are described, but diagnosis may be challenging when these findings are absent. We describe a dog with peri-anesthetic acute bilateral blindness in which conventional MRI was equivocal, but advanced MRI supported suspected GBI by demonstrating global hypoperfusion, reduced arterial conspicuity, and microstructural alterations.

CASE PRESENTATION: A 10-year-old intact male Welsh Corgi was noted to have acute bilateral blindness at discharge after overnight hospitalization following general anesthesia at a local hospital and later deteriorated neurologically. On examination, mentation was depressed; menace responses were absent bilaterally with intact pupillary light reflexes. Brain MRI revealed diffuse cerebral swelling with sulcal effacement and no conspicuous supratentorial T2W or FLAIR hyperintensity or clear diffusion restriction. Advanced MRI supported suspected GBI: arterial spin labeling showed markedly reduced cerebral blood flow (CBF) in the cortex and thalamus; dynamic susceptibility contrast showed reduced relative CBF, increased relative cerebral blood volume, and prolonged transit metrics; time-of-flight magnetic resonance angiography showed poor intracranial arterial conspicuity; and diffusion tensor imaging demonstrated cortical alterations in fractional anisotropy and diffusivity metrics. Mannitol and supportive care were administered; blindness persisted and the dog was discharged with poor neurologic recovery.

CONCLUSIONS AND RELEVANCE: Multimodal MRI may support the diagnosis of canine GBI when conventional MRI is equivocal and may characterize global hemodynamic and microstructural injury.
