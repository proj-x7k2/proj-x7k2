# Clinical characteristics of pneumorrhachis in dogs and cats: a retrospective study of 125 cases.

**Journal of veterinary internal medicine (2026)** · 후향적 연구, n=125 · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42855434/) · DOI: 10.1177/1098612X251385878
관련 주제: [[topics/spinal-imaging]]

## 국문 요약
Vertebral canal 내 gas로 정의되는 pneumorrhachis(PR)의 임상적 의미와 vacuum phenomenon(VP)과의 관계가 체계적으로 평가된 적 없다는 문제의식에서, 2015~2023년 CT에서 PR이 확인된 개 119마리·고양이 6마리를 후향적으로 분석했다. 개에서 PR은 60/119마리(50%)에서 neurological 질환과 동반되었고 그중 83%(50/60)가 intervertebral disc disease(IVDD, Hansen type I 64%)였으며, IVDD 관련 PR 중 46%(23/50)에서 VP가 동반되고 그중 78%가 병변 disc와 같은 위치에 있었다. Non-neurological 질환은 45/119마리(38%, 주로 neoplastic·respiratory)에서, 수술 후 PR은 12/119마리에서 확인되었고, 원인불명 PR은 2마리(2%)였다. 고양이에서는 PR이 neurological(2/6)·non-neurological(2/6)·수술 후(2/6)와 연관되었고 IVDD·VP 관련 예는 없었다.

## English summary
Pneumorrhachis (PR), gas within the vertebral canal, is uncommon and its clinical relevance and relationship to the vacuum phenomenon (VP) had not been systematically evaluated. This retrospective study characterized PR distribution on CT in 119 dogs and 6 cats (2015-2023). In dogs, PR occurred with neurological conditions in 60/119 (50%), mostly intervertebral disc disease (IVDD, 83%; Hansen type I in 64%), with VP present in 46% of IVDD-related PR (78% co-localized with the affected disc). Non-neurological conditions accounted for 45/119 (38%, mainly neoplastic and respiratory), and PR occurred postoperatively in 12/119 dogs; 2 dogs (2%) had PR without identifiable cause. In cats, PR was associated with neurological (2/6), non-neurological (2/6), and postoperative (2/6) conditions, with no IVDD or VP cases.

## 임상 시사점
CT에서 pneumorrhachis가 발견되면 먼저 IVDD(특히 Hansen type I)와 vacuum phenomenon 동반 여부를 확인하되, PR 자체는 흔히 incidental finding이므로 neoplastic·respiratory 질환 등 non-neurological 원인과 수술 후 상태도 함께 감별해야 한다.

## 연구 설계와 한계
2015~2023년 CT 영상의학 판독 보고서에 PR이 기록되고 임상·영상 정보가 확보된 예만 포함한 후향적 서술 연구다. 고양이 표본이 6마리로 매우 작아 개와 동일한 결론을 적용하기 어렵고, 선택 바이어스(판독 보고서에 PR이 명시된 예만 포함)가 있을 수 있다.

## 원문 초록
BACKGROUND: Pneumorrhachis (PR), defined as gas within the vertebral canal, is an uncommon finding, and its clinical relevance and relationship to the vacuum phenomenon (VP) have not been systematically evaluated.

HYPOTHESIS/OBJECTIVES: To characterize the anatomical distribution of PR in dogs and cats undergoing computed tomography (CT) and to assess its relationship with signalment, VP, and concurrent neurological and non-neurological conditions.

ANIMALS: One hundred nineteen dogs and 6 cats with PR identified on CT between 2015 and 2023.

METHODS: Retrospective, descriptive study of medical records and CT reports. Cases were included when PR was documented in the radiology report, clinical and imaging information was available. Data included signalment, PR distribution, VP presence, final diagnosis categorized as neurological, non-neurological, postoperative, or no identified cause.

RESULTS: Pneumorrhachis was identified with neurological conditions in 60/119 dogs (50%), including intervertebral disc disease (IVDD) in 50/60 (83%). Among IVDD-related PR, Hansen type I occurred in 32/50 (64%) cases. A VP was present in 23/50 dogs (46%), co-localized with the affected intervertebral disc in 78%. Non-neurological conditions accounted for 45/119 (38%) dogs, mainly neoplastic (44%) and respiratory (36%), with VP infrequently detected. Pneumorrhachis occurred postoperatively in 12/119 dogs. Two dogs (2%) had PR and epaxial muscle pain without an identifiable cause. In cats, PR was associated with neurological (2/6), non-neurological conditions (2/6), and postoperatively (2/6). No cases involved IVDD or VP.

CONCLUSIONS AND CLINICAL IMPORTANCE: Pneumorrhachis in dogs and cats is considered an uncommon CT finding occurring with neurological and non-neurological conditions. Although often accompanying IVDD in dogs, PR was frequently incidental.
